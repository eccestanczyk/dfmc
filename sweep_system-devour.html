† DEVOUR

Other Systems › Devour

One creature consumes another — or a Devour item — and takes what was worth keeping. The fodder's XP always transfers in full. Grade and mutant status transfer too, but only between the same species. The fodder is destroyed permanently: its uid is burned and nothing recovers it.

Where it lives

  • Soul bag: open a creature's sheet — † DEVOUR sits beside DISMANTLE. That creature is the consumer.
  • Equipment window: click an equipped creature's slot — † DEVOUR sits under ATTEMPT UPGRADE. The equipped creature is the consumer, and its gains (level, grade → QM, mutant) re-derive the party immediately.
  • The panel shows the consumer on the left with the arrow pointing at it, and the fodder slot on the right. Clicking the slot opens the picker.
  • The main view fits without scrolling; only the fodder PICKER scrolls, because that one is a list of every eligible creature in the bag and no layout fits 100+ rows. Sized from measurement: the two cards are min-height:276px with 11px padding, the body has 16px 24px padding and a 12px gap, and the preview line is 15px/1.35. Body content is 359–380px against a panel that caps at 593px on a phone, so there is real room before a scrollbar can return. LIVE on release since 2026-08-05 (0.32).
  • Why the phone scrolled and the desktop did not. The overlay is position:fixed, which escapes the stage’s CSS zoom, so its max-height:88% resolves against the REAL viewport rather than the 1080-tall stage: 88% of 674px = 593px on a phone against 792px on a 900px desktop. Every other size in this panel is a stage unit. Any fixed-position overlay in this build has the same asymmetry — measure it at a phone viewport, not just at desktop.
  • Devour only opens on the floor or in the boss room. During a battle (select through flash) it refuses with NOT DURING A BATTLE. While the panel is open the floor simulation is frozen, like every other window.

What can be consumed

The fodder picker lists the soul bag's visible pool only: creatures and Devour consumables. By construction this means anything equipped, penned or socketed can never be fodder — those are not in the visible pool. Eggs and class souls are never fodder. The consumer is excluded from its own pool; a creature cannot devour itself. Consumables sort to the top of the picker, then creatures in soul-bag order.

Devouring a creature

AspectRule
XPAlways transfers, in full, regardless of species: consumer.xp += fodder.xp. Level is re-derived from the shared XP curve.
GradeTransfers within the same LINE, and only upward: the consumer keeps the higher of the two grades. Any stage feeds any other stage of its own line. Grade feeds the Grade Multiplier, so this is a real stat change.
MutantTransfers within the same LINE, across stages: a mutant fodder makes the consumer a mutant (see Mutant Variants). Never removed, never reversed.
FodderDestroyed. The record leaves the bag, the uid is burned, equipment is unaffected (a bag creature carries none).
IdentityThe consumer's uid, origin, gender and everything not listed above are untouched. It is the same creature, fed.

“Same” means the same LINE (D ruling 2026-08-02). Identity for grade and mutant is Line_ID from creatures.csv, so an S1 and the S3 it advances into ARE the same for this rule: feed a Stage-1 Rare mutant into its own Stage-3 Common and the Stage-3 comes out Rare and mutant. Across different lines only XP moves.

The comparison falls back to the creature row when creatures.csv has no row for a record, so an unknown or forged creature with a blank Line_ID can never read as “same line” as everything else. dvSpeciesId still exists and is still exact-creature identity — it answers a different question and the picker labels off it.

The Devour consumables

Three item types live in the soul bag under the new Consumable category. None of them can be obtained anywhere yet (D ruling) — no drop, no craft, no purchase mints them; mkDevourItem is the single mint and nothing calls it in play. A refused consumable is never spent.

ItemEffect when consumedRefusal rule
Blood PelletGrants XP to the consumer. Currently 1,000 XP — a placeholder constant (DEVOUR_PELLET_XP) pending tuning; each pellet record can also carry its own xp.None.
MutagenThe consumer becomes a mutant.Refused if the consumer already is one: ALREADY A MUTANT.
[Grade] Essence of PowerRaises the consumer's grade to the essence's grade (e.g. an Abyssal Essence of Power makes the creature Abyssal). Order of operations

  • One judgement function (dvJudge) computes the outcome for both the preview line and the execution, so what the panel promises is exactly what commits.
  • The commit is one atomic state update: fodder removed (or its stack decremented) and consumer rewritten in the same write. If the world moved underneath — the fodder left the bag, the slot changed occupants — the updater bails and nothing is paid.
  • The consumer is resolved live on every read: a bag consumer must still be in the bag, a slot consumer is whatever the slot holds now.
  • Each successful devour writes a DEVOUR adventure-log event and saves the run. The panel stays open so the player can keep feeding the same creature.

Edge cases and consequences

  • Advancement is not triggered from the panel. Devoured XP can carry a creature past its advancement level, but the advancement scene plays where it always does: a party creature advances on its next victory; a bag creature advances once it is equipped and wins. The XP is banked either way.
  • A grade raise on an equipped consumer changes its quality multiplier at once — the party re-derives and the power number moves.
  • Devour only ever removes from the bag, so the inventory guard is not in play — no path here returns an item.
  • Stacked consumables decrement quantity; the last one leaves the bag.

Integrity

Devour resolves entirely client-side, like crafting and grading: the result rides the save, and the server does not yet validate creature XP, grade or the mutant flag. It therefore belongs to the same anticheat class as those systems — the Phase 2b transaction gate (server-rolled outcomes) covers it when it lands. Until the items are obtainable, nothing of economic value is minted here: a devour can only concentrate what the account already legitimately holds into fewer creatures.

The fodder picker shows what it cannot take

Creatures that are equipped (a party slot or a soul socket) or breeding (in a pen) appear in the fodder list, greyed, badged EQUIPPED or BREEDING, and cannot be selected. Clicking one says why rather than doing nothing.

  • They sit after the selectable creatures. Sorting them in among the rest would push a creature the player can feed below three they cannot, on the one screen whose entire job is picking one.
  • They are shallow copies, never the live records. Nothing that reaches this list can be fed, but a copy cannot be fed by accident either.

The report that prompted this was a name collision, and the measurement is worth keeping. It was raised as “it is showing mons currently in breeding as options”. Compared by uid on the reporting save, zero penned, equipped or socketed creatures were in the list — penning and equipping both splice the creature out of the bag, which is exactly why isPennedItem() and isEquippedMon() can honestly return false. The account held three creatures called Tempest and two called Ingot; one of each was penned, and the rest were ordinary bag creatures. What the player could not do was tell them apart.

So the filter that was asked for already existed. Showing the unavailable creatures greyed is what fixes the actual confusion, and it is worth more than the filter: a list that silently omits a creature cannot explain where it went.

Client: dvBlockedRecs(), dvChoices() in play/app.js. Probe: tools/probe_devsel.py — BEFORE 4/4 (asserting the creatures are absent entirely), AFTER 7/7, matching by uid rather than by name.

If none of them moves, the transaction had no content — and until now it went through anyway, destroying the fodder for literally nothing and reporting success. A freshly hatched creature carries zero experience, so feeding one across lines, where grade and the mutant flag cannot transfer at all, was the ordinary way to hit it. The devour is now refused with “THERE IS NOTHING TO BE GAINED HERE” and the fodder survives.
    The refusal is deliberately narrow. Experience alone still pays. A grade rise still pays even when the fodder carries no experience, and so does a mutant passing on. And GRADE HELD is untouched: that is a real devour which paid experience and merely failed to raise the grade, so it still goes through and still says so. The consumables were already correct and are not changed — a mutagen refuses a creature that is already a mutant, an essence refuses unless it outranks, and a pellet's experience falls back to the standard value so it can never be zero.
