VOID APEX

Other Systems › Void Apex

The postgame zone above floor 100 — twenty floors of maxed-out wild creatures and ten über bosses behind consumable keys. Best rewards in the game, no shortcuts to reach them.

Shape of the zone

  • Zone 11, floors 101–120. Unlocked by clearing floor 100.
  • Odd floors spawn, even floors are boss rooms — ten grind floors (101–119) alternating with ten über bosses (102–120).
  • Fast travel like any floor. The floor map lists the Apex floors like every other zone: TRAVEL on an opened grind floor, ENTER DEN on a conquered boss floor. Progress persists — the highest Apex floor reached is kept; this is not a gauntlet that restarts each session.
  • Any creature in the game can spawn on any Apex grind floor — the Floor Map lists them all.
  • 30 wins per grind floor opens the boss room above it — a flat count, where the main tower's count rises floor by floor.
  • Autoplay behaves normally on every Apex floor, grind and boss alike.
  • Wild creatures stay at level 100. Everything that makes an Apex creature stronger comes from its quality, its gear and how it fights.

Wild creature tuning

Quality, gear and battle AI climb with every grind floor. Floor 101 picks up where floor 99 leaves off; floor 119 is a pure-Void creature with full gem and rune sockets. Boss floors have no wild creatures.

Quality grade

Picks up from floors 91–100 (Epic 40 / Legendary 60) and marches to pure Void at 119. This is the enemy's quality only: it makes the creature stronger and does not change what drops.

Gear

Every Apex wild carries two built-in tags at tier 10 — a Fang (Attack) and a Carapace (Defense) — graded at its own quality grade. Each tag holds as many gems and runes as that grade has sockets (see Sockets), all at the same grade: Attack gems and runes on the Fang, Defense ones on the Carapace. Wilds carry no enchants and nothing that adds HP.

AI tiers

Apex enemies choose their moves and targets instead of picking at random. The tier is set by the floor, every über boss and its actives fight at tier 3, and each tier keeps everything the one below it does.

Tier 1

Focus the lowest-HP target. Floors 101–103.

Tier 2

Adds Anti-clause exploitation and holds high-cooldown moves for kill windows. Floors 105–111.

Tier 3

Adds priority targeting of the player's utility creature and status-opener sequencing. Floors 113–119, and every über boss.

  • Lowest HP (every tier) — attacks aim at the foe with the least HP left, ties in slot order.
  • Kill window (tier 2+) — a ready attack that kills a foe even on the low damage roll is always taken first, the lowest-cooldown one that does it.
  • Held moves (tier 2+) — an attack with a cooldown of 3 or more is saved for a kill window unless nothing else is ready.
  • Anti (tier 2+) — a ready attack whose Anti clause names a living foe's type is played into that foe.
  • Status opener (tier 3) — a debuff whose status a foe does not carry yet goes before plain damage.
  • Utility creature (tier 3) — the foe with the most support and debuff moves is focused first.

Everywhere else — the tower below floor 101, Auto PVP and Competitive — enemies keep picking at random.

The dice are never bent. The 0.9–1.1 damage roll, accuracy and every other chance stay exactly as they are at all tiers — difficulty comes from stats, kits and decisions.

Drops

Every Apex drop rolls one of four grades — Rare, Epic, Legendary, Abyssal. Rare is the floor: nothing worse ever drops in the Apex. Divine, Primordial and Void never drop and remain upgrade-only. Drop rates match the main tower: gear, enhancements and eggs each drop in about 10% of battles, and every boss kill guarantees one piece of gear and one egg.

Each Apex floor has its own grade odds, rising floor by floor. On a grind floor, gear and eggs roll on that floor's odds and enhancements on the floor below's (floor 101 uses its own). An über boss's gear rolls on the next floor's odds (the floor-120 boss on its own) and its egg on the boss floor's. The boss floors:

Eggs

  • Grade band Rare–Legendary. Eggs roll the same odds, but an Abyssal roll becomes Legendary — so the Legendary-grade egg chance rises instead: 3.1% on floor 101, 29.6% on floor 119, 33.5% from the floor-120 boss. No Abyssal eggs; breeding stays the only path above Legendary.
  • Rarity climbs too. The spawn mix shifts toward SR and SSR with depth — floor 101 continues floor 99's mix, and SSR grows from 12% of spawns to 50% by floor 119. A wild egg is the defeated creature's own egg and rarer creatures drop eggs more often, so the egg mix follows: SSR share of wild eggs 29.6% → 68.2%.
  • Über boss eggs. One egg per über-boss kill, and it can be any line in the game, not one of the creatures in the fight. Its rarity is drawn on the boss floor's own weights (the main tower's bosses use 30/30/20/10/10): 12/18/25/22/23 at boss 102, ramping to 2/5/13/30/50 at boss 120 (C/UC/R/SR/SSR). The line is then drawn evenly within that rarity, and the grade rolls on the boss floor's odds, capped at Legendary.
  • Yield, simulated: a dedicated player (12 h a day) pulls ~18 SSR eggs a day mid-zone and ~25 a day at the cap, of which roughly 1.5 and 7.4 respectively are Legendary grade.

Über keys

  • One key per boss (“Void Apex Key — Vargr” and so on). A grind floor drops only the key for the boss directly above it — farm 109 to fight 110.
  • Spent when the boss fight starts, not on victory. A loss burns the key; walking into the den and out again costs nothing.
  • A grind floor starts dropping its key once you have 30 wins there. From then on each creature you defeat rolls for it, rarer creatures a little likelier, at most one key per battle. The server rolls it, so a reload cannot re-roll it. Rate: expected one key per ~360 battles, about 12 hours of Apex play.
  • Pure roll — no pity, no timer, no guaranteed key. Half of all keys arrive inside ~8.3 h; one in ten takes over ~27.6 h; one in a hundred over ~55 h. Unlike the main tower, clearing the floor below a boss gives no free key.

Über bosses

The ten über bosses are the tower's own ten bosses, returned in mutant form, in the order they were first met — Vargr on floor 102 through Eskaton on floor 120. Each comes back in mutant colouring at level 100 with Void quality, higher grades, tier-3 AI and one signature rule that has to be solved rather than out-statted. Each keeps its original four moves, with the same powers, cooldowns and synergy; the step up is in grades, HP, gear, AI and the signature rule. Its two actives return as their final evolved forms, at Abyssal quality on floors 102–104, Divine on 106–108, Primordial on 110–112 and Void from 114. Boss and actives carry built-in gear graded Legendary on 102–104, rising one grade every two bosses to Void on 118–120.

Loot: no per-boss tables. Every über boss draws from the same Apex pool as everything else in the zone — what separates them is only the grade odds on their floor. There is no target-farming a particular boss for a particular item; the reason to climb is that the odds get better.

Each über portrait is the mutant recolour of the original boss's art.

Signature rules in battle

A signature rule is a status on the boss, visible from the moment the fight starts, on the buff side of its plate. It carries a red screaming skull for an icon and dark grey and black parchment, so it cannot be mistaken for an ordinary buff. Hovering it shows the rule in full, ending with “This cannot be removed.” It never expires, never stacks and cannot be cleansed.

Look

The Apex floors are broken stone ruins adrift in a starfield. A violet and cyan fog drifts over them, a wisp on floor 101 and thick by 120. Every Apex enemy wears an aura that grows stronger floor by floor, its colour stepping up one grade per grind floor — Worn on 101 to Void on 119.

The Infinite Abyss — floor 121 and beyond

The postgame challenge above the summit: a climb with no top, fought against floor 119's creatures.

  • Entry. No floor-map row. The only door is a portal in the floor-120 summit den, sealed until über Eskaton is down. Entering lands on the Abyss at 121.
  • The number. The title reads THE INFINITE ABYSS with the number large beneath it. Every 100 consecutive wins adds 1, forever. Only a death sends it back to 121 — a lost Abyss fight (or a forfeited one). Leaving the Abyss (its one door, LEAVE THE ABYSS, returns to the 120 den; any other floor change too) steps out of it but keeps the number and the streak: entering again resumes them. A PvP match or a reload does not touch them either, and a Competitive match waits until your current Abyss fight is over.
  • The skull. Under the streak box (top right, ABYSS STREAK n / 100) a large skull; hovering it states these rules and the current monster uplift.
  • Monsters. The floor-119 pool, always. Every stat — Attack, Defense, HP and Speed — is +1% per Abyss floor, compounding: ×1.01(n−121).
  • Rewards. Floor 119’s, always — XP, Ichor, drops, keys, eggs. A challenge mode, not a farm.
  • Background. From 121 the plate walks the tower in order — 121 is floor 1’s art, 122 floor 2’s, … 240 floor 120’s — under a darker, hue-rotated filter; at 241 it laps back to floor 1, darker and stranger again, one step per lap. The Apex fog is off in the Abyss.
  • Rankings. The deepest number reached is written to the Highest Climb board, so an Abyss run outranks the summit; the board reads it as Abyss N.
