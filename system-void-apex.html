VOID APEX

Other Systems › Void Apex

The postgame zone above floor 100 — twenty floors of maxed-out wild creatures and ten über bosses behind consumable keys. Best rewards in the game, no shortcuts to reach them. Design settled 2026-08-10 (D rulings); not yet implemented.

Shape of the zone

  • Zone 11, floors 101–120, shown as corrupted numerals (???1 … ??2Ø). Unlocked by clearing floor 100.
  • Odd floors spawn, even floors are boss rooms — ten grind floors (101–119) alternating with ten über bosses (102–120).
  • No fast travel. Apex floors never appear in the map selector; movement is portal-only and sequential. Progress persists — the highest Apex floor reached is kept, this is not a gauntlet that restarts each session.
  • No creature list. Apex floors publish no spawn roster anywhere in the client or codex, and any creature in the game can spawn on any Apex floor. What appears where is community knowledge, not a table the player is handed.
  • 30 wins per grind floor opens the boss room above it — a flat threshold, replacing ceil(floor^0.65) inside the Apex.
  • Autoplay behaves normally on every Apex floor, grind and boss alike.
  • Wild creatures stay at level 100 — the cap is not raised. Everything that makes an Apex creature stronger comes from its build.

The optimization rule — deterministic, no per-species authoring. Every gem, rune and enchant on an Apex wild takes that species' dominant axis: Grade_Attack ≥ Grade_Defense → Garnet / Ruin / Attack enchants, otherwise Onyx / Aegis / Defense. AI tiers

Tier 1

Focus the lowest-HP target. Floors 101–103.

Tier 2

Adds Anti-clause exploitation and holds high-cooldown moves for kill windows. Floors 105–111.

Tier 3

Adds priority targeting of the player's utility creature and status-opener sequencing. Floors 113–119, and every über boss.

The dice are never bent. The 0.9–1.1 damage roll, accuracy and every other chance stay exactly as they are at all tiers — difficulty comes from stats, kits and decisions. Battles are deterministic replays, so a biased roll would be provable from a log anyway.

Drops

Every Apex drop rolls one of four grades — Rare, Epic, Legendary, Abyssal. Rare is the floor: nothing worse ever drops in the Apex. Divine, Primordial and Void never drop and remain upgrade-only. Each drop reads its own floor's row; boss loot reads the boss floor's row. Existing rates are untouched: three independent 10% rolls per battle, and two guaranteed drops per boss kill.

Eggs

  • Grade band Rare–Legendary. Eggs use the same curve with the Abyssal share folded into Legendary — so the Legendary-grade egg chance rises instead: 3.1% on floor 101, 29.6% on floor 119, 33.5% from the floor-120 boss. No Abyssal eggs (D ruling 2026-08-10); breeding stays the only path above Legendary.
  • Rarity climbs on two levers. The spawn mix itself shifts toward SR and SSR with depth (codex/encounter_weights.csv (rows 101–120)) — floor 101 continues floor 99's live mix, and SSR grows from 12% of spawns to 50% by floor 119. Since a wild egg is the defeated line's own egg and the roll is rarity-scaled, the egg mix follows automatically: SSR share of wild eggs 29.6% → 68.2%.
  • Boss eggs re-authored (codex/apex_boss_egg_rarity.csv), replacing the main tower's 30/30/20/10/10: 12/18/25/22/23 at boss 102, ramping to 2/5/13/30/50 at boss 120 (C/UC/R/SR/SSR).
  • What the boss egg is (build mechanics, on DEV since 2026-08-27, on release at the next push). One egg per über-boss kill, rolled server-side in the same keyed stream as every other drop, in this order: (1) the pool is the zone's own line list — every line with a spawn row on floors 101–120, i.e. all 100, each line carrying the rarity tier authored for it on floor 101 (the first Apex row that names it); (2) a tier is drawn from that boss floor's row in apex_boss_egg_rarity.csv, normalised over the tiers present; (3) a line is drawn uniformly inside that tier; (4) the grade rolls on the boss floor's own band and clamps to the Legendary wild-egg cap. The egg is any line in the game, not one of the three creatures in the fight (D ruling 2026-08-27: “apex boss is any egg ok”). A boss floor without a row in the Apex file — every tower boss — keeps boss_loot.csv's 30/30/20/10/10 and its own zone. Before 2026-08-27 the build looked the pool up by the clamped item tier (10), so every Apex boss egg was a Flaming Hellscape line at the tower weights and the Apex file was read by nothing; the live grant log showed 8 of 8 Apex boss eggs as zone-10 lines.
  • Yield, simulated: a dedicated player pulls ~18 SSR eggs a day mid-zone and ~25 a day at the cap, of which roughly 1.5 and 7.4 respectively are Legendary grade.

Über keys

  • One key type per boss, authored in codex/items.csv as ITM-UKEY-102…ITM-UKEY-120 (“Void Apex Key — Vargr” and so on, tier 10, level 100, art prompt_ready). A grind floor drops only the key for the boss directly above it — farm 109 to fight 110. Climbing is what adds new key types to the pool.
  • Consumed on boss entry, not on victory. A wipe burns the key.
  • Per-kill roll, rarity-scaled and normalized like zone keys, rolled and debited server-side. Rate: expected one key per ~12 hours of Apex play (~360 battles).
  • Pure roll — no pity, no timer, no guaranteed key (D ruling 2026-08-10). Half of all keys arrive inside ~8.3 h; one in ten takes over ~27.6 h; one in a hundred over ~55 h. There is no guaranteed-key equivalent of the floor-9 zone rule.

Über bosses

The ten über bosses are the tower's own ten bosses, returned in mutant form. Not new characters — Vargr comes back on floor 102 and Eskaton on floor 120, in the same order they were first met, each in mutant colouring at level 100 with Void quality, uplifted grades, tier-3 AI, and one signature rule that has to be solved rather than out-statted. Their two actives return with them at Void quality and Apex-grade virtual gear. Kits are inherited from the source boss and re-tuned; art is a mutant recolour of the approved boss portrait, so no new character design is needed. Data: codex/bosses.csv (rows 102–120).

HP is budgeted from the live boss anchor (an on-progression party kills a floor-100 boss in ~10 turns at ~490 damage per turn). Apex budgets assume a party doing 2× that at floor 102 rising to 5× at floor 120, targeting 14–16 turns throughout — 2.8× the floor-100 boss at the entry and 8× at the cap.

Loot: no per-boss tables (D ruling 2026-08-10). Every über boss draws from the same Apex pool as everything else in the zone — what separates them is only the grade odds on their floor's row. There is no target-farming a particular boss for a particular item; the reason to climb is that the odds get better.

Portraits are done and cost nothing. Each über portrait is the deterministic mutant recolour of the approved boss art, produced by tools/make_mutants.py — the same hue-cluster remap that makes every creature mutant, seeded from the boss id, luminance untouched. Run it with python3 tools/make_mutants.py codex/images/mutants bosses; output is codex/images/mutants/BOSS-0X0.webp, exactly where anything already looks for the mutant of that id. No image generation, no matte pass, no credits.

Move powers are deliberately NOT inflated. The authored power bands top out at 160 (Nuke, CD3–4) and every one of these bosses already sits on that ceiling, so the über kits carry over from bosses.csv verbatim — same four moves, same powers, same cooldowns, same synergy text. Raising them would put numbers outside the band system every other move in the game is balanced against. The uplift lives entirely in grades, HP, AI tier and the signature rule. Kits and synergy strings are copied into codex/bosses.csv (rows 102–120) so the über row is self-contained and can be retuned later without touching the tower boss. Overturn this if you want über-only powers above the band.

Probed on a real worker 2026-08-10 — 14/16. The server half is verified. Floor 119 pays, with grades from the authored Apex band only, item tier 10 so the eleventh zone clamps correctly, and no Divine or above. Floor 102 is a boss floor and 101 is not — the retired every-tenth rule had both backwards — and a floor-102 kill grants exactly the guaranteed gear and egg its loot table authors. The floor is not clamped to 100: floor 100 authors zero drop chance, so a drop at 119 could not have come from a clamped floor. Floor 1 is unaffected. The single remaining failure is the probe running out of rate-bank credit, not the game. A probe must run the worker with ENV_NAME:dev or it reads the pinned codex and measures the previous release.

Four hardcoded assumptions block the Apex. Each is small, and each is a place where today's code restates “the tower is 100 floors of 10-floor zones” instead of reading it:

  • Client — DONE on dev 2026-08-10. The 100-floor tower was written into the client in 25 places. TOP_FLOOR replaces the travel ceiling; bossAt() reads Is_Boss from floors.csv (already loaded) so a boss on every even floor is expressible at last; winsToClear() reads the authored Wins_To_Clear instead of restating ceil(floor^0.65) in four places. Both helpers fall back to the retired rules only when the sheet has not loaded, because the client fails open on a failed CSV fetch. The Apex is reachable and its 30-win threshold is live on dev.
  • Client — wild virtual gear: FIXED on dev 2026-08-10. wildTags() derived the tag tier as ceil(floor/10), which is 11 at floor 101 and 12 at 119; no Tag item exists at those tiers, so the lookup missed and every Apex wild would have carried zero virtual gear, silently. Tier now clamps at 10. Note the standing limit this exposes: a wild's tag is a single implicit scaled by tier and quality grade, and wilds have no gem, rune or enchant sockets — so above floor 100 the tag value is flat and quality is the only lever that still grows enemy gear, which is exactly what the quality ladder is doing.
Data note — there is no second copy. Every Apex table lives in this repo and nowhere else, exactly like the rest of the game data. Dev reads live main, so Apex tables are visible on /dev/ as soon as they are committed; release reads the immutable DATA_PIN snapshot, so nothing here can reach live players until a release advances the pin. That is why the design and its data can land in full while the code is still unwritten.

Signature rules in battle

Built and live on dev 2026-08-10. A signature rule is not a hidden script — it is a status on the boss, visible from the moment the fight starts, sitting on the buff side of its plate like anything else happening to a unit. It carries a red screaming skull in place of the usual effect icon, and its parchment is recoloured dark grey and black so it cannot be mistaken for an ordinary buff. Hovering it shows the rule in full, ending with “This cannot be removed.” There is no duration line, because it never expires, never stacks and cannot be cleansed.

ENFORCED, all ten, on dev since 2026-08-10. The engine reads the rule and acts on it. Handlers hang off four hooks — incoming damage, the killing blow, the end of a round, and the door every named status passes through — plus one bracket in the action ordering. Authored per boss row as Signature_Name and Signature_Rule; a boss whose rule has no handler is simply a boss with a chip, so nothing breaks by being unauthored.

Server authority

The most valuable content in the game is also the first content built server-authoritative end to end: the key drop roll, the key debit on entry, the Apex floor clear, and every über reward are all server-side, granted only against an open server-held boss session. A disconnect pauses and resumes through the deterministic replay; no refund, no offline credit. All five Apex tables ship pinned like every other data file.

The tower is 120 floors — the travel clamp, the autoplay gate and the board bound

Shipped in 0.46 as a silent hotfix on 2026-08-10. LIVE on release. Void Apex raised the tower from 100 floors to 120: floors.csv authors floors 1–120 and TOP_FLOOR = 120 is declared in both play/app.js and src/worker.js. Three places still restated the retired 100-floor tower as a literal, and each one is its own failure.

1. travel() clamped to the old ceiling — players were hard-stuck on floor 100

travel() computed its target and then clamped it with Math.min(100, target), left over from the 100-floor tower. The order of operations is what made this fatal rather than merely wrong:

  • Floor 100 is the Godforge boss den, so leaving the den computes 101.
  • The clamp pulls 101 back to 100 — a boss floor, which has no wild map.
  • The target === floorNum no-op guard on the next line then compares 100 against 100 and returns. Every further ascend was refused for the same reason, permanently.

The observable symptom is a portal that draws, accepts the click and does nothing at all — no error, no toast, because the guard is a legitimate no-op path and reports nothing. 0.46 had already moved the portal LABEL to TOP_FLOOR and left the clamp behind, so the tower advertised a floor on a button that could not reach it. This was an incomplete migration, not an unattempted one, which is why the surface looked correct.

2. The autoplay advance gate carried the same literal

Autoplay's advance test read fn < 100 — the same retired constant written a second time in a second place — so autoplay stopped dead at exactly the floor the travel clamp stuck on. Both sites now read TOP_FLOOR.

3. Leaderboard plausibility rejected an Apex climb

The plausibility check bounded a climb entry at 1–100, so an Apex player's board write was refused as lb floor: a legitimate score against a stale bound. Widened to TOP_FLOOR.
