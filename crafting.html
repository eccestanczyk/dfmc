CRAFTING

Nothing is useless. Every drop dismantles into materials. Every material crafts the next tier.

Recipes are authored, not formulaic. Every craftable item has its own row in codex/recipes.csv with an explicit material bill. There is no cost formula anywhere in the crafting system — change a recipe by editing its row. Output grade defaults to Worn — the forge can deliver up to Rare for a surcharge derived from the grading ladder at the recipe's tier (2026-07-31), so forging is never cheaper than grading it yourself; the climb beyond is the separate Scrap-fed gamble on the Upgrading page.

Raising an item's grade

There is no combining. Gear grade is raised only by spending resources on an attempt — Scrap for gear, Dust for gems, Essence for runes, Ink for scrolls, all authored in codex/grading.csv at the item's tier (360 tier-scaled rows, 2026-07-31). A failed attempt consumes the currency and leaves the item exactly as it was; nothing is ever destroyed. Those are the same resources dismantling yields, so every drop feeds the ladder. See the Upgrading page for the full table.

Scroll rerolling

There is no separate reroll action and no Ink cost. Applying another scroll over a socketed one is the reroll — it draws a fresh class and value from codex/enchants.csv, and the enchantment it replaces is destroyed. Scrolls can never be unsocketed; gems and runes always return to the bag intact.

Scroll legality (2026-07-31): a scroll is a die, not a fixed stat — the socket stat-match rule judges its rolled result. A flat Attack/Defense/HP roll must match the host stat exactly like a gem; item-local % rolls are host-relative by definition, and Increased/More/utility rolls and unrolled scrolls fit any host. (Before this ruling the catalog text was unreadable as a stat, so every socketed scroll in the game read ILLEGAL — bug #3.)

Socket identity (2026-07-31): every equipped item is its own instance — two copies of the same item never share sockets. Unequipping or swapping an item returns its gems and runes to your bag (D ruling 2026-07-31); only the scroll stays bound to the item, travels with it, and returns to its socket when the item is equipped again. Dismantling a socketed item refunds its gems and runes the same way — the scroll is destroyed with it. Promoting an equipped item carries only that copy’s scroll across the grade.

The dismantle result — a panel, and why it had to become one

A completed dismantle opens a centred list panel: one row per resource acquired, a socketables-returned line where sockets came back, and a close button. It is contained by construction — an inset:0 overlay centring a card whose width is capped in percent of its container, so no yield string can push it past the edge however many currencies it names. The rows are derived from the currency set, so a currency added later appears without touching the panel.

It replaced a single-line toast, and the toast was a real defect. The line was white-space:nowrap, and a full sweep names five currencies plus a refund count. Measured on a 430 px phone: that line drew 963 px wide and pushed documentElement.scrollWidth from 430 to 1178. A document wider than the viewport is answered by the browser zooming out — which is exactly what “the notification is resizing the game window” describes. Even the short breeding-egg line overflowed, at 582. The toast itself now sizes to its content with a percent cap and wraps, so it can no longer widen the document whatever text it is handed.

Overlays belong to the stage, not to the device. Six panels were position:fixed, which escapes the zoomed 1920×1080 stage and sizes them against the device viewport instead. An authored 1020 px card then becomes 94% of a phone while its children keep their authored pixel widths — which is why the devour window scrolled sideways: three flex:none cards at 330 + 66 + 330 plus gaps need roughly 770 px in a box near 400. The devour row now shares whatever width exists (flex:1 1 0 with min-width:0) and keeps its authored size wherever there is room, so a desktop is unchanged.

Client: dismDone and dismDoneRows in play/app.js; the results panel and the devour row in play/markup.html. Probe: tools/probe_popupfit.py (8/8, measured on a 430 px viewport).
