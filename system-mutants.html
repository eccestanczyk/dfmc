MUTANT VARIANTS

Other Systems › Mutant Variants

Any egg can hatch as a Mutant, and any wild creature can spawn as one — a creature with an alternate colour scheme from a rare pigmentation mutation. It is purely cosmetic: a medal on the creature sheet and a recoloured palette, with no effect on stats or gameplay.

Mutant chance by grade

Chance scales with egg grade. Completing the Codex adds a permanent flat bonus to every mutant chance, in two halves (zone tiers and Void lines, below).

Wild spawns

Mutants are not a hatch-only phenomenon. Every wild creature rolls for Mutant colouring when it spawns, on exactly the same odds — the table above, read against the wild creature's own grade instead of an egg's grade, and lifted by the same Codex zone tiers. A Void-grade wild spawn is a Mutant 0.10% of the time (0.30% with every zone tier and every Void stamp); a Worn one, 0.01%.

A wild Mutant is a sighting: it fights in its recoloured palette and marks the Codex accordingly. It is still cosmetic — identical stats, moves, and grade to any other spawn of its kind — and, like every wild creature, it is not itself Acquired; Acquisition happens by hatching.

Codex completion tiers

The Codex bonus is not one all-or-nothing unlock — it is earned zone by zone. Each of the ten zones has its own Codex tier: Acquire (hatch) every creature that appears on that zone's floors and the tier locks in permanently.

Each zone tier grants a permanent +0.01% flat Mutant hatch chance. All ten zones = the full Codex = +0.10%, exactly the completion bonus in the table above — the reward was always +0.1%; it is now paid out in ten instalments instead of one.

The Void Apex (floors 101–120) is an eleventh zone and counts as a tier of its own, but the zone half is capped at +0.10%, so it is a spare: any ten of the eleven complete the half.

Zones overlap by design. Creatures appear on floors across several zones (one creature can be found on floors 1, 11, 21 and 32), so each zone's set holds 69–87 creatures (the Creatures column above) and the ten sets union to every line that spawns on floors 1–100. A creature counts toward every zone tier whose floors it appears on — completing the Grasslands tier therefore also carries you part-way through the tiers above it, and the ten tiers finish together only when the last creature is Acquired.

Acquired means hatched. Seeing a creature in the wild marks it Seen in the Codex but does not count toward a tier. A stage 1 counts when you hatch it, and each later form when a creature you hatched evolves into it.

How it behaves

  • Cosmetic only — grants a medal and a recoloured palette; identical stats, moves, and grade to a normal hatch.
  • Codex completion bonus — two halves, both permanent and added to all grades. Zone tiers: +0.01% per completed zone tier, +0.10% for all ten. Void stamps: +0.001% per creature line stamped Void (a line is stamped the first time you hold a Void-grade creature of it, and the stamp stays if you later dismantle it), capped at +0.10% for all 100 lines. Together +0.20%, which lifts the Void ceiling to 0.30%. Both halves apply to hatches and to wild spawns.
  • Never dismantled — a Mutant cannot be dismantled: the creature sheet has no dismantle button for it, the hammer refuses it and says why, the by-grade and all-filtered sweeps skip it, and Adam and Eve leaves it alone — neither dismantled nor kept as a bucket’s keeper.
  • Devour carries it forward — a creature that devours a Mutant becomes a Mutant itself (nothing changes if it already was one). Fusion is no longer in the game; Devour is the one way the trait moves between creatures.
Even at the Void ceiling the base rate is 0.10% (1 in 1,000), so Mutants stay a genuine rarity flex. Because devouring passes the trait on, a single Mutant hatch can seed more than one recoloured creature.
