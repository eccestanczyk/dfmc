0 — Core formulas (revised)

Creature stat (Atk/Def):  Stat = (Core + Σ TagStats) × AxisMult × QM
                              Core = 1.6 × (level + 25)     AxisMult = (3 × axisGrade + 5) / 20
                              (identical naked values to the old (g×30+50) curve — pure reframe)
Creature Speed (flat):             Speed    = axisGrade          (1-10, equals axis grade)
Creature HP:                       HP       = (4 × level + 35) × (5 + DefenseGrade) / 11 × 7.5
Creature quality multiplier:       QM       = 0.80 + 0.04 × qualityGrade      (applies to Atk/Def/HP — NEVER Speed)

Character Attack:   (Σ ItemStats(Attack)  + 0.25 × Core) × ClassRating × RiskPremium + 188   ClassRating = (3 × stageStat + 5) / 8
Character Defense:  (Σ ItemStats(Defense) + 0.25 × Core) × ClassRating + 188   (+188 = innate floor, post-multiplier)
                    ClassRating never falls below 1.00 (grade 1 ×1.00, grade 5 ×2.50, grade 10 ×4.375), so gear always delivers at least its printed value.
                    RiskPremium = 1 + 0.15 × (6 − DefenseGrade): the low-Defense classes are paid in damage for dying sooner.
                    Characters carry 25% of Core as a level term — before July 2026 a character's Attack did not scale with level at all.
Character HP:       (4 × level + 35) × (5 + stageDefStat) / 11 × 7.5 × 1.5 × (1 + 0.12 × (6 − stageDefStat)) + Σ flat HP items
Character Speed:    Grade_Speed for the class stage (classes.csv) — a per-class, per-stage value like Attack and Defense, never a constant. No gear sources; only in-battle stat stages move it.
Boss HP:            5 × Floor + 150   (explicit budget per boss in bosses.csv — not the creature HP formula)

Damage:  RawDamage   = ((2 × Level / 5 + 2) × MovePower × Attack / Defense) / 130 + 2 + Attack / 50
         FinalDamage = RawDamage × Synergy/Anti × Random(0.9–1.1) × (1 + Σ increased%) × Π(more)
         No same-type damage bonus and no type-effectiveness multiplier — there is no type chart.
         Type interaction lives only in each move's Synergy and Anti clauses.
The earlier Battle System formula (g×6+2)×L/100+g×3 was inconsistent with its own level-100 table; the revised base-stat formula above reproduces the committed table exactly (grade×30+50 at level 100) and keeps grade ratios level-invariant. HP was re-curved linear-in-level so turns-to-kill stays constant while leveling.

Stat aggregation (multiplier model). ItemStat = (implicit + Σ gems + Σ Flat enchants) × (1 + Σ rune % + Σ Increased% enchants) × (1 + (LOCAL soul − 1) + Σ More% enchants). Scrolls roll ONE enchantment on apply, from codex/enchants.csv: class weights Flat 45 / Increased 40 / More 15, value uniform inside the class band, and the roll can only take the stat the host item itself carries — a Defense item never rolls Attack. Flat = 3.6 × T × GF (roll ×0.55–1.45); Increased = 5.6% + 1.70% × (g−1) + 0.56% × T (roll ×0.55–1.45); More = 12.0% + 0.90% × (g−1) + 0.50% × T (roll ×0.60–1.50). More is additive with the item's LOCAL soul multiplier rather than a separate factor. Duplicates are allowed and stack inside their bucket. There is no separate reroll action: applying another scroll over a socketed one rerolls it, drawing a fresh class AND value, and the enchantment it replaces is destroyed. Scrolls can never be unsocketed; gems and runes always return to the bag intact. Expected value per socketed scroll is about +12% of the finished item at T3 Uncommon and +18% at T10 Void; three maximum More rolls on a Void item reach +78%, flat enchants add into the base band. Character: Σ ItemStats × ClassAxisMult + 10 where ClassAxisMult = (3 × stageStat + 5) / 20 — Paladin Def 10 → ×1.75; the +10 is an innate floor applied after the full chain (July 2026). Creature: (Core(level) + Σ TagStats) × AxisMult(grade) × QM with Core = 1.6 × (level + 25). Damage divisor 130, random band 0.9–1.1, floor 2 + Attack/50. Validated fully-maxed: striker vs char wall 15. MAGNITUDE REWORK + BALANCE PACKAGE (July 2026): every item implicit in items.csv was multiplied by 3 and the class multiplier by 2.5 (denominator 20 -> 8), a combined stat scale of 7.5; Core, the innate floor, HP and the damage constants (divisor 130 -> 17.33, flat 2 -> 15) all scale with it, so the rework alone leaves combat identical and only makes the numbers legible. On top of that: Core k 1.6 -> 1.0, tag implicits x3 again (3.0 -> 27.0 total), the innate floor 10 -> 25 pre-scale, the character level term and risk premium above, character HP x1.5 with the squishiness term, and a wild Attack tag budget from floor 11.1, creature mirror 16.0, HP-stacked creature wall 19.3.

Heart — flat HP

110 × tier × GF, about 2× the Fang / Carapace grid — multiply the table above by 2.04. A point of Creature Defense is simply worth more than a point of Creature HP: the Defense tag is added to Core before the axis multiplier (1.0–4.375) and quality, while the HP tag is flat. At the old equal coefficient, measured in effective HP against the real damage roll, Carapace beat Heart by 1.46× to 2.66× at every level and every Defense grade tested, so Heart was never a pick. The doubled coefficient buys parity in EFFECTIVE durability, not double the wall: at Defense grade 9 the two are within a point of each other at Lv 40. They now trade places on two axes — low-Defense creatures and early floors favour Heart (grade 2: +40% vs +29%; Lv 10: +17% vs +8%), high-Defense creatures and deep floors favour Carapace (Lv 80: +26% vs +21%). Revised 2026-07-27; the previous rule was that HP is no cheaper per point than any other stat, which is what made Heart a dead item.

Creature axis grades do not rise with stage
parked 2026-08-02, D: "put this in the balancing backlog, not doing it right now"		 Not a missing implementation — an authored asymmetry. Reported by a player as "stats stay the same between stages". Verified three ways. DATA: creatures.csv carries one grade set per LINE, not per stage — Stormfather is 10/4/3/9 at stages 1, 2 and 3, and 99 of 100 lines are identical at every stage (mean Grade_Attack 5.66 / 5.69 / 5.69). CODE: monFromSlot() captures stage and never uses it for a stat; the [0, 0.6, 0.8, 1] stage ladder exists only for souls. CODEX: creature Atk/Def is (Core(level) + ΣTagStats) × AxisMult(grade) × QM with no stage term, while CLASSES get a per-stage row (Prophet 2/5/5 → 2/7/7 → 3/8/9), and the balance notes state that a creature being a stage ahead shows up in move power. Code and codex agree.

If it were taken: giving creatures class parity (+3 attack grades S1→S3) runs through AxisMult = (3g + 5) / 20 as a same-level buff of +64% (grade 3→6), +45% (5→8), +35% (7→10) and +0% for anything already at 10 — so it also compresses the top of the ladder. That is a 300-row creatures.csv change with a DATA_PIN advance in the same commit, and large enough to want tools/balance_*.py run before and after.

codex/proposed/creature_stage_grades.csv		 The rule, calibrated from CORALHYDRA rather than invented. Its authored S1 sits at axis(6)/axis(9) = 0.719 of S3 on Attack and axis(5)/axis(8) = 0.690 on Defense — so S1 is 0.70 × the S3 multiplier, and S2 is the midpoint, 0.85 ×, each snapped to the nearest grade on the same AxisMult = (3g + 5) / 20 curve. Fed back through the rule, CORALHYDRA's S1 comes out 6 / 5 exactly, which is what makes this a derivation and not a guess.

Current grades are kept as S3 for every line, per D. Attack and Defense are the only axes that move: Utility is flat in CORALHYDRA, and Speed must stay flat because the GDD defines it as ungeared, unlevelled and unaffected by quality — a per-stage Speed would silently reorder turns on advancement.

Why not a flat grade delta. CORALHYDRA's S1 is literally S3−3, but the grade distribution is bottom-heavy (23 lines at Attack 3, 27 at Defense 3), so a flat −3 would collapse 63 of 100 lines to the grade 1 floor and erase the role signal that a low axis grade carries. Scaling the multiplier instead clamps only 2 of 100.

Result: mean S1→S3 Attack gain ×1.42 (min ×1.00 on the two floored lines, max ×1.55), landing beside the +45% class ladder rather than on top of it. 199 of 300 rows change; all 100 S3 rows are untouched by construction.

One thing to rule on: CORALHYDRA's authored S2 is flat with S3 (9/8), so its second advancement currently pays nothing. The rule gives it 7/7, making the ladder monotonic and every advancement felt. Taking the rule changes CORALHYDRA at S2 only; keeping the authored value means the last advancement is a move-power upgrade with no stat gain.
