Boss entrance

A boss is not entered like a wild encounter. Pressing the challenge in the den plays a themed entrance first, and the battle only becomes playable when it finishes. It is deliberately longer than the wild intro — roughly 4.2s against 2.65s — and it is skippable with a click at any point.

Sequence

StageAtWhat plays
00.0sThe screen drops to near-black behind a radial veil and the whole overlay shakes three times. The boss art sits at 15% opacity, oversized, waiting.
11.1sThe sigil ignites: an N-pointed star in the boss's own type colour, scaling up from 55% and turning slowly, with a contracting ring around it. 110 particles burst outward in the same palette. The boss art rises to full opacity.
22.4sThe art settles from ×1.14 to ×1.00 and starts breathing. The name plate drops in: floor number, boss name, and its type line.
—4.2sThe entrance ends and the fight begins; the action menu opens 0.4s later.

A reload during the entrance never traps you in it, and a click anywhere skips straight to the fight.

Boss names

Every boss has a full name with its title, e.g. Mother Vesk, the Broodmother, shown on the den screen, the entrance card and in the codex, and a short name, e.g. Vesk, on the battle name plate. The den has a name of its own, e.g. The Broodmother’s Hollow. The Void Apex bosses are Über Vargr on the den plate, the floor map and the battle plate, and Über Vargr, the Alpha on the den screen, the entrance card, the codex and the key — never “Mutant”.

Respawn — the gravestone

Re-entering a boss room does not respawn the boss. After it dies a gravestone stands in its place and counts down from 10 seconds; at zero the boss returns in place. Keys are the rate limit, not the timer — the gravestone is a beat, not a gate.

  • The 10-second window is kept per den, so beating one boss never hides another.
  • Only elapsed time counts: walking out and back changes nothing.
  • While it counts, the den shows the countdown and its button reads THE ALPHA STIRS…. At zero the boss returns in place and the button reads ⚿ CHALLENGE THE BOSS, or REQUIRES A BOSS KEY when you hold none.

After a boss win

A boss floor is a den only: it has no wild map and no spawns of its own. A boss win leaves you standing in the den with the gravestone counting in front of you. The boss floor counts as cleared and the victory screen names it; a bug report filed in the den names the den's floor. Leaving the den returns you to the floor below it, and travelling up from the den lands on the floor above.

Reaching a den

  • The approach floor. On the floor just below a boss, once its portal is open, walk in.
  • The floor map. Fast travel straight to a den you have already conquered.
  • The key's own button in the key's item details, beside DISMANTLE: ENTER THE FLOOR N BOSS takes you to that den from anywhere in the tower. It shows once you have reached the floor below that boss, so it also works for a boss you have not beaten yet.

None of the three spend the key. The key is spent when you challenge the boss, not at the door — walking into a den and leaving costs nothing.

Which key a boss takes

A boss on floor B takes the key that drops across the nine floors below it — B−1 down to B−9. The floor-10 boss takes the key found on floors 1–9, the floor-100 boss the key found on 91–99. Keys do not drop on boss floors themselves. The first time you clear the floor just below a boss, you are always given one key for it.

Keys are strictly per zone. A key opens the boss it belongs to and no other.

The key count is the boss's. The number shown, the challenge gate and the key actually spent all count the keys for the boss being challenged, wherever you are standing.

All ten of these bosses come back. The Void Apex postgame zone (floors 101–120) fights them again in mutant form, in the order they were first met — Vargr on floor 102 through Eskaton on floor 120 — at level 100 with Void quality, higher grades, tier-3 AI and one signature rule each. They keep their four moves, their actives return as their final evolved forms, and their portraits are the mutant recolours of the art here.

What a boss drops — and the odds, in the game

Every boss kill drops one piece of gear and one egg, rolled by the server. The gear's grade rolls one floor band ahead of the boss's own: the floor-10 boss rolls the floors 11–20 odds, the floor-100 boss floor 101's, an über boss the next floor's, and the floor-120 boss its own. Divine and above never drop. The egg is a line from the boss's zone, weighted by rarity 30/30/20/10/10 (C/UC/R/SR/SSR; the über bosses use their own weights, see Void Apex), with its grade rolled on the boss's own band and capped at Legendary. The full tables are on Droptables.

The game shows the same numbers itself: the CODEX window opens with a BOSS DROPS strip for the next boss on your climb — the guaranteed counts, each grade with its percentage for the band the boss rolls, and the egg-line split — the way the hatch screen shows its odds. It is a display only; the roll is the server's.
