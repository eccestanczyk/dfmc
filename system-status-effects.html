STATUS EFFECTS & BUFF STACKING

Other Systems › Status Effects & Buff Stacking

Every buff and debuff in the game belongs to exactly one ability. Two different abilities stack; one ability never stacks with itself. Magnitudes on the same stat combine additively, never multiplicatively, so a +50% and a +15% is +65% — not ×1.725.

Identity: a status is its ability

A status is identified by the ability that applied it, not by the stat it moves. Every buff a move applies carries that ability’s identity and its stage name (Stormcall / Call the Storm / Convocation of Storms), which is the name shown on the status chip.

Every buff clause of the move — its self buff, its ally buff, its single-target ally buff and its Synergy clause — carries that identity, so all clauses of one ability count as one status. Riders, marks and DoTs applied to enemies keep their authored names (Mothervenom, Impale) and are identified by that name.

Stacking rules

SituationResult
Two different abilities buff the same statBoth statuses exist side by side. Their magnitudes add.
Two clauses of the same ability land on one unitOne status. Same ability, so they merge — they do not stack.
The same ability is cast againDuration refreshes to the new cast. Magnitude keeps whichever is stronger. Never a second instance.

"Stronger" means further from the baseline in the direction the payload points: 1.0 for multipliers, 0 for flat adds. A +50% Attack buff re-applied at +35% stays +50%; a −40% Attack debuff re-applied at −25% stays −40%. The chip takes the name of the cast whose magnitude survived, so a weaker later cast cannot relabel a stronger running buff.

This is what caps buff spam. Feed the Conflagration is +50% Attack for 3 turns on Cooldown 1; without same-ability merging, every new cast would pile another +50% on top of the last.

Aggregation and clamps

Every status on a stat adds its change, the total is applied once, and the result is held between ×0.30 and ×2.50:

Attack multiplier = 1 + (sum of every status’s change), between ×0.30 and ×2.50

  • Attack and Defense follow the formula above.
  • Speed is a flat add: stages sum directly, no multiplier, no clamp beyond the stage system.
  • Damage taken follows the same additive rule and the same 0.30–2.50 clamp, summed over the target's statuses at hit time.
  • Because deltas sum, a buff and a debuff on one stat cancel point for point: +50% and −30% is ×1.20.

Named statuses with no number of their own apply fixed changes: Slagbite and Fracture −22% Defense, Gravechill −18% Attack and −2 Speed, Cinch −3 Speed, Doomrune +18% damage taken, Warded −18% damage taken.

Synergy clauses and the caster

A move's Synergy clause is a second buff clause gated on creature type. Two rules govern whether the caster is included:

  • A type-gated buff includes the caster when the caster is of that type. A Dragon casting "Dragon allies gain +15% Attack" is a Dragon and receives it. This is not a special case — the caster is an ally.
  • A clause that requires another ally of a type excludes the caster from that test. "If no Undead ally, self +30% Attack" asks whether some other party member is Undead; the caster does not satisfy its own requirement.

Because the Synergy buff is the same ability as the move's own self buff, a caster who qualifies for both gets one status at the stronger of the two magnitudes. A weak type synergy can never overwrite the ability's own stronger self buff.

Worked example — Convocation of Storms

The Stormfather line’s fourth move at its third stage: targets the caster only, Self +50% Attack, 3t, Synergy Elemental and Dragon allies gain +15% Attack, 3t. Party: Charybdis (Dragon·Elemental, the caster), Ignifer (Dragon·Elemental, already running its own Feed the Conflagration at +50%), and a Construct character.

Targeting

Every move has a fixed target — the caster, one ally, or the enemy side — never guessed from its power. It decides what you can select as well as what the move hits:

  • Self-only moves — only the caster is highlighted and only the caster can be clicked.
  • Support moves that reach one ally highlight the living allies.
  • Hostile moves highlight the living enemies.
  • An illegal click is refused. It is never silently redirected to a legal unit.

Boss signature mechanics cannot be removed

A boss’s unique rule is carried as a status with the skull icon, and its tooltip reads “This cannot be removed.” It lasts the whole fight:

  • Remove-buff, steal-buff and buff-shortening effects never touch it — they pass over it to the next buff.
  • A round rule that clears the party’s buffs (The Unfinished Work) does not clear a signature status either.
  • It still counts as a buff everywhere else: it shows in the buff row and satisfies “target has a buff” conditions.
  • A boss’s own written counterplay still works — Sethek’s second rising is cancelled by killing his escort first.

Thorns — what is reflected

Thorns comes from two places and both follow one rule: a status on the unit (from a move that reads “attackers take N% of their damage back”, such as Bulk of the Living Mine) and souls (the character’s own thorns souls, plus team thorns on every ally). Each fires once per hit taken and deals its share straight to the attacker’s HP, shown on the damage line as “N reflected”. Retaliation ultimates are a different mechanic and are not thorns.

  • The share is of the damage the unit actually lost on the hit — after its own defence, and never more than the HP it had left. A tank that shrugs a hit down to 100 reflects a share of 100.
  • Both sources follow this rule, and a multi-hit attack counts every hit.
