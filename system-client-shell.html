CLIENT SHELL & PLATFORM RENDERING

Other Systems › Client Shell

How the game fits itself onto a screen. The whole UI is authored on one fixed 1920×1080 canvas and scaled to the device; the shell page around it owns loading, fullscreen, scroll pinning, platform quirks, and pushing every connected client onto a new build. These behaviours live in the hand-maintained shell, not in the generated screens.

The stage & text fitting

  • One canvas, one number — the stage is a fixed 1920×1080 box. On boot and on every resize the client computes fitScale = min(viewportWidth/1920, viewportHeight/1080) and applies it to the stage as CSS zoom. Roughly 0.92 on a desktop monitor, ~0.36 on a phone held landscape.
  • Aspect is preserved — one uniform factor for both axes; a mismatched screen letterboxes rather than stretches. Portrait phones letterbox heavily, which is why fullscreen carries an orientation lock (below).
  • Text fitter, horizontal — every element styled with text ellipsis is measured after each render; if its text overflows its box by more than 1px, its font shrinks proportionally, floored at 11.5 stage px (one retry step lower at 11). The original size is cached and restored when the text fits again.
  • Text fitter, vertical — a fixed-height card marked as a fitbox shrinks all of its text together in 6% steps, down to a floor of 72% of authored size (min 11 stage px), instead of clipping.
  • Stage units, not screen units — all fitter floors are in stage px and render at fitScale of that on screen. Sizes chosen for readability must be chosen in stage units with the phone scale in mind.

Platform: iOS — the stage fit (2026-09-07). LIVE on release since 2026-09-08 (0.71).

What was actually wrong. The warped iPhone UI was not text autosizing. WebKit applies its built-in 9 px minimum logical font size to the zoomed size: under the stage’s zoom: 0.32 every font authored below 9/0.32 = 28 px renders at 28 stage px, and the browser reports that inflated size back as the computed size. Two consequences: every small label, socket cell and caption on an iPhone drew 2–3× too big for its box, and the text fitter re-read the inflated size as its base on every render and wrote it back — a multiplier per render, which is how the class card’s stat digits reached ~15× in D’s screenshot. Chrome has no such minimum, which is why Android was never affected. Reproduced in a real WebKit engine (Playwright WebKit 26 emulating an iPhone), not reasoned from source; gate tools/probe_iosstage.py 14/14, red 5/14 before.

  • The fix: no zoom on iOS at all. The shell widens the layout viewport (width = max(1920, 1080 × screenW/screenH)) so the 1920×1080 stage sits at zoom 1 and Safari’s own viewport scale shrinks the page to the screen — the same path every desktop-width site takes on an iPhone. Fonts lay out at their authored size, metrics are exact, touch coordinates are native, and the app’s fitScale comes out at 1. Recomputed on every visual-viewport change (toolbar collapse, rotation, keyboard).
  • A CSS transform was tried first and rejected — it made WebKit rasterise the whole stage at device resolution every frame (9 s per frame at 3× in the harness). The gate now also checks that frames keep arriving.
  • The text fitter is hardened for every platform — its base size is the authored inline size, captured once per node; no browser-side multiplier (a minimum, a text zoom, autosizing) can compound through it any more.
  • Touch feedback off on iOS (board 10.1, 2026-09-07). LIVE on release since 2026-09-08 (0.71). The “transparent square instead of the reticle” report: in the same WebKit engine the target rings render as masked rune circles under both the old and the new stage, so the mask and the zoom are out; what remains is the browser painting its own feedback over whatever is tapped — the tap highlight (a 40 % black rectangle in WebKit, inherited by every tappable element), the long-press callout, and the text-selection highlight under the 450 ms holds. All three are now off for the game on iOS; inputs and the bug-report box stay selectable. Gate tools/probe_iostap.py 9/9 on the exact surfaces named (portal, floor creature, battle unit, boon tile).
  • Still gated to iOS by ruling — the block returns before touching anything unless the shell’s iOS tag (below) is set; desktop and Android render byte-for-byte as before (the gate’s desktop control checks the meta and the zoom).

Platform: iOS text autosizing (the 2026-08-23 gate, kept)

2026-09-07: this was the wrong diagnosis for the warp — see the section above — but the switch is harmless and stays; the tag it introduced is what the stage fit keys on.

iOS Safari inflates font sizes on its own (“text autosizing”), and it boosts explicitly-set pixel sizes too. That breaks the text fitter's contract — the size the fitter writes is not the size that renders — so the fitter measures boosted text, shrinks it, Safari boosts the result, and the cycle repeats on every render. The visible symptom is a warped UI: text bursting out of boxes that are themselves correct. Desktop browsers never autosize, and Chrome on Android stands its boost down on pages with a device-width viewport meta (this page has one), so the fault is iOS-only.

  • The fix — text autosizing is switched off (text-size-adjust: 100%) so the fitter's number is the rendered number.
  • Gated to iOS by ruling — D ruled (2026-08-23) that desktop and Android must not change in any way, so the rule only applies when the shell has tagged the page as iOS. No tag, no rule — every other platform renders byte-identically to before.
  • The tag — set in the shell head, synchronously, before first layout: the platform reports as iPhone/iPad/iPod, or reports as Mac while the device has a multi-touch screen. iPadOS 13+ masquerades as a Mac but keeps its touch points; no real Mac has a touchscreen. Touch-screen Windows laptops and all Android devices report neither form and never match.
  • Third-party iOS browsers are covered — Chrome, Firefox and Edge on iOS are WebKit underneath, report Apple platforms, and warp the same way; they get the fix.
  • The future app wrapper inherits it — an iOS app shell is a WKWebView: same engine, same platform strings, same tag, nothing to reconfigure.
  • Fails safe — an exotic browser reporting neither form gets no tag and renders exactly as today.

Scroll pin

  • The document itself must never scroll, not by one pixel — a scrolled document slides the centred stage off-screen and takes corner controls with it while fixed-position elements stay put.
  • Two guards: the stage wrapper is viewport-anchored (fixed) so it cannot drift, and the shell resets any document scroll offset to 0 on every scroll and resize event. overflow:hidden alone does not stop programmatic or focus-driven scrolling.

Fullscreen & orientation

  • A fixed corner button toggles fullscreen. On handheld devices only (coarse pointer, no hover — touch-screen laptops excluded), entering fullscreen also requests a landscape orientation lock, because the 16:9 stage letterboxes a portrait phone to a stamp. Exiting fullscreen releases the lock. Platforms without orientation lock (iPadOS Safari) still get fullscreen.

Build watcher

  • Every connected client polls its own environment's build fingerprint every 45s (release watches release, dev watches dev). On a new build: out of battle the client saves and reloads itself; anywhere a silent reload would cut across live play, the game halts under a full-screen refresh button instead.

Opening a unit’s sheet in battle

Status: the three-tap route is LIVE on release since 2026-09-02 (0.67). Two ways in, one rule for every pointer:

  • Hold a living unit for 450 ms (mouse). The release click is swallowed so the hold never also selects or targets.
  • Three taps on the same living unit, each within 600 ms of the last (mouse or touch). The first two still do what a tap does — select an ally, pick a target — and the third opens the sheet instead. Taps on two different units never add up.

Why two: a phone cannot hold. Touch browsers deliver the compatibility mouse-down and mouse-up together after the finger lifts, so the 450 ms timer is cancelled the instant it starts and a phone had no way into a unit’s sheet mid-fight. The rule is not device-sniffed; a desktop triple-click reaches the sheet too.

ESC closes the current window (board 9.13, 2026-09-07). LIVE on release since 2026-09-08 (0.71).

One layer per press, innermost first, and it stops there: a confirmation before its window, a creature sheet before the bag under it, a picker before the equipment screen, a sub-window before its parent, then the main window, then the menu, then the letter. Every close goes through the window’s own close routine, so nothing a click would clean up is skipped. In a fight ESC only backs out of a target pick — it never leaves combat. It does nothing while an upgrade attempt is in flight or settling, while a trade is in flight, while a hatch answer is in flight, on the skull card (OK only), during character creation, or during a floor transition. With a text field focused (the bug report, the name claim) the first ESC only leaves the field; the next one closes the window. The Settings hotkey capture keeps its own ESC. Gate tools/probe_esc.py 13/13 with real key presses, red 0/13 before.

2026-09-24 — the Tower has sound (D). ON DEV (0.85). This client had no audio at all: move_vfx.csv’s AFX_Archetype / AFX_Layer / AFX_Pitch columns had been read into the client’s move table since 2026-09-10 and nothing ever played them, and there was no AudioContext anywhere in the build. There is one player now, AFX_BANK, a sibling of the VFX bank’s block and built the same way — it depends on nothing else in the client, so the codex’s review page can lift it verbatim and hear exactly what the game hears. It reads three authored tables: afx_bank.csv (the 606-clip CC0 bank, shared verbatim with the mystery dungeon), afx_events.csv (one row per event cue) and bgm.csv (ten zone beds plus hub, boss and title). A move’s sound is a composition in move_vfx.csv’s AFX_S1/AFX_S2/AFX_S3, one per stage, in the grammar of AFX_SPEC.md; an ultimate carries AFX_S1 alone. THE STANDING AUDIO RULES ARE ENFORCED BY THE PARSER, NOT BY TASTE: a clip with a fifth of its energy above 3 kHz is refused unless it is pitched at least three semitones down, a clip that peaks above 0 dBFS is refused unless its gain is cut to 0.7, and the gains of layers landing inside one 60 ms window may not sum past 1.6 — what reaches the ear is the sum, not the loudest member. “Never use annoying high pitched sounds, deeper is better and more comfortable” [R D 2026-09-14] is a measurement here, and the bank carries the measurements. A move with no composition, or a file that does not load, plays NOTHING — there is no synthesised stand-in and never was. Low VFX, Battery Saver and Reduce Motion thin the picture and do NOT silence the sound: audio has its own mix. Twenty event cues are wired, each from one place: the button press, an overlay opening and closing, a refused action, a toast, the start of a fight, the win, the loss, a unit falling, a level-up, the advancement ceremony, a hatch, an egg laid, a craft that landed and one that did not, a drop, a purchase, the boss arriving and falling, and the ascent. The music bed follows the screen rather than the code: a hub window owns it, a battle takes its floor’s zone, a boss floor takes the boss bed, and the title bed waits for the player’s first click, because no browser will start audio before one. VOID APEX REUSES THE ZONE BEDS TWO FLOORS AT A TIME [R D 2026-09-24]: 101-102 play the first zone, 119-120 the tenth. The zone ordering is read from floors.csv, so it is authored in one place and restated nowhere. Beds crossfade — 1200 ms in, 900 ms out — and a screen change that does not change the bed never restarts it. Gate tools/probe_afxbank.mjs, beside the VFX bank’s own: it lifts the player out of the shipped client, runs it against a Web Audio stub, and checks a 9-item legal and 27-item illegal corpus, the whole contract, the fold rule, and every authored composition and referenced file against the codex. 0/2 before, 46/46 after.
