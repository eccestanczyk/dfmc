EQUIPMENT, THE GEAR LOCK & AUTO-OPTIMIZE

Other Systems › Equipment & Auto-Optimize

Equipment can be changed at any time, including in the middle of a fight. What it cannot do is change a fight already in progress: every combat bonus is snapshotted when the battle starts and served from that snapshot until the battle ends.

The gear lock

  • All equipment-derived combat values — flat and percentage stats, souls, thorns, heal-received, debuff potency and duration, type and kind multipliers — come from gearBonus().
  • At the start of every battle the current bonus is computed and frozen. For the rest of the battle gearBonus() returns that frozen copy and ignores the live loadout entirely.
  • The freeze is taken at four entry points, each of which begins a battle: a wild encounter, a boss challenge, a re-entered fight from the victory screen, and a battle resumed after a page reload.
  • Creature and character unit stats (HP, attack, defence, speed, moves) are separate snapshots taken by initUnits() at the same moment, so they behave identically.
  • Out of battle the lock does not apply and the live loadout is authoritative immediately.

The lock binds the FIGHT, not the SHEET (D ruling 2026-08-02). Every display surface reads the live loadout: the character stat panel, POWER, and the creature cards all update the instant an item is swapped, mid-battle or not. Only the combat path sees the frozen copy. Before this the freeze applied to everything, so a player who swapped a weapon during a fight saw no number move anywhere on the equipment screen and had no way to tell the swap had taken. Display producers are wrapped in a helper that lifts the freeze for the duration of the read and restores it on the way out — including on a throw, so a render can never leak live gear into the fight in progress.

Why it is taken deliberately and not lazily. The freeze used to happen on whichever combat read called gearBonus() first. That was only safe while the equipment window and the optimizer both refused to run during a battle — once the battle gate came off, a player could change their loadout before any combat read and the NEW gear would have been frozen instead of the old, silently altering the fight in progress. The snapshot is now taken at battle entry, before anything can act.

Which stat an item grants

An item's stat comes from its own row in items.csv, never from the slot it sits in. itemStat() reads the Effect column and matches the authored grammar (+flat X for gear, tags and gems; increased % X for runes). The slot's default stat is only a fallback for an item with no effect row at all.

This matters most for accessories, which are the one category where the family decides the stat:

FamilyEffectGrants
Signet+Attack (implicit, 6×T×GF, Local)Attack
Relic+HP (implicit, 9×T×GF, Local)HP

Both go in the same two Accessory slots, whose slot-level default is Attack — so any surface that labels off the slot instead of the item will call a Relic an Attack item while showing its correct HP number. Weapons and armour hide this class of bug, because there the slot default happens to match the item.

Auto-optimize

  • Searches the equipped items plus everything in the bag and proposes the loadout with the highest character Power, reshuffling gems and runes globally across the result. Scrolls never move.
  • It touches gear slots only. The two creature slots (Monster I and II) are outside its order and are never changed by it — see The Active Party.
  • It runs during a battle (D ruling 2026-08-02). The change lands immediately and, because of the lock above, takes effect from the next battle.
  • The preview is re-derived and compared before it is applied; if anything moved in between it is refused with “Things changed — run it again” rather than applied against a stale plan.
  • If the resulting loadout needs more bag room than the account has, it is blocked before anything moves.

Scoring must bypass the lock. The optimizer measures a candidate by swapping it into state and asking the game for its own Power number. During a battle gearBonus() would answer with the frozen snapshot for every candidate alike, so every option scored identically and the optimizer reported “already optimal” whatever was in the bag. The measurement therefore lifts the freeze for the duration of the swap and restores it afterwards. This was found by probe the moment the battle gate was removed — the gate had been hiding it.

Client: gearBonus(), freezeGear(), optBuildPlan(), optScore(), optApply() in play/app.js

The soul picker

Ordered grade > level > line. The list built its cards straight off BAG.concat(bagAdd) — insertion order — so a Divine sat wherever it happened to have been hatched and the player had to hunt for it. It now sorts by GRADE_ORDER descending, then level descending, then line, then name.

  • Line is the third key, not the first. Grade decides the slot, level decides between equals, and the line only groups what is otherwise identical. Name breaks the final tie so the order cannot shuffle between re-renders on equal creatures.
  • Same comparator shape as the breeding picker and the inventory, which is the point: three lists that order creatures should not each invent their own order. The breeding selector had this exact defect on 2026-08-05.
  • BAG is copied before sorting. It is not React state, and the bag's own order is its identity — sorting it in place would reorder the inventory as a side effect of opening a picker.
  • Hovering a candidate shows its move summary. The rows always set eqHMv; eqHoverOn carried !s.eqPick, so the summary was suppressed precisely while a picker was open — the one screen where a player most needs it was the one screen that refused it. Fixed 2026-08-05.
  • The containment rule is not abandoned, only excepted. The original rule — “never paints over a picker or a confirm” — still holds for sock picks and confirms. Over a SOUL picker the panel is allowed, and two things make that safe rather than a straight reversal: it is pinned to top:82% so it does not sit on the list being hovered, and it is pointer-events:none so it cannot steal the mouse off a row. Without the second, hovering would drop the hover the instant the panel appeared, and flicker.
  • Each row states the soul's strength as the live stage × grade figure. It previously read “60% strength” from the retired stage ladder — a number that stopped existing when the surface was rebased to ×1.00–×2.50 earlier the same day. Anything quoting a multiplier reads it from the same place the maths does.

Probe: tools/probe_soulhover.py (BEFORE 4/4, AFTER 5/5). It dispatches a real mouseover on the row, because the renderer strips sc-camel-on-mouse-enter from the DOM and there is no attribute to select on. LIVE on release since 2026-08-05 (0.32).

The passive text states what the game pays

souls.csv authors the S3 text, and gearBonus then applies p.<pct> × stMult × grMult. The screen quoted the CSV constant while the game paid something else — measured on a live save, the equipment UI read +4% Attack while gearBonus was accumulating 6.22. The percentages in the text are now the effective ones.

  • Rewritten in place, not appended. The sentence still reads as a sentence — PERSONAL: +6.2% Attack. — rather than +4% Attack. → +6.2%. A player reading a passive is reading a description, not a derivation. soulEffLine still builds the derivation for the details window, where showing the working is the point.
  • Only the magnitudes scale, and parseSoul decides which those are. Scaling every percentage in the sentence was the first attempt and it corrupted the condition: +5% Defense while above 50% HP became above 77.8% HP, silently changing when the soul fires. The magnitudes are taken from parseSoul's own output rather than from a rule about plus signs, because parseSoul is what gearBonus pays out of — a number it does not read as an effect is a number the game does not scale either.
  • The multiplier has left the equipment UI (D ruling on report #47). The tile badge and the picker row's ×1.56 strength are gone: those quote the lever, and a player choosing a soul can do nothing with a lever. The details window now carries ×N STRENGTH (Sstage × grade) along with the raw → effective working for every slot in the line.
  • Freeing the tile also freed the advancement arrow, which sat off-centre at top:32% to avoid the multiplier band. It is back to dead centre at full size.

Client: soulTextEff(), soulEffLine(), invTipInfo() in play/app.js. Probe: tools/probe_soulpct.py — BEFORE 3/3 (asserting the text quotes the raw constant), AFTER 9/9, with the displayed number compared against what gearBonus accumulates rather than against a formula the probe restates.

The soul readout

Each of the eight gear slots shows a readout of what its soul grants — the move for an ability slot, the passive text for an armour slot. Since 2026-08-05 that panel also reports the slotted creature, not only its effect:

  • An XP bar fills the bottom edge of the readout, and the creature’s current level sits bottom-right (26px stage, 9.34 CSS px on a phone).
  • These are live. A socketed soul is a creature and it levels — battleRewards banks XP into soulOv[gi].src — so the bar is read through monXpOf/xpLevel, the same pair the creature sheet uses. The bar and the sheet cannot disagree, and neither is a snapshot taken when the creature was consumed.
  • Gender appears on the soul tile and on the party creature card. Resolved with monGenderKnown, never monGender: the latter defaults to M so breeding always has a side to compare, and printing that default as fact is exactly what the UI must not do. A creature with no stamped gender shows no glyph.

Probe: tools/probe_eqsoulxp.py (BEFORE 4/4, AFTER 6/6). The party card carries its own level label at 15px stage — 5.39 CSS px, still sub-legible and not yet raised; see The Stage. LIVE on release since 2026-08-05 (0.32).

Soul strength — stage × grade

A gear soul’s output (passive magnitude, and granted move power on the four ability slots) is scaled by two multipliers that multiply together. They are set as one curve, because neither is meaningful alone.

LeverRangeWhat it costs
Source creature’s stageS1 ×1.00 · S2 ×1.12 · S3 ×1.25 Raised by advancing the creature — levels, not resources.
Source creature’s quality gradeWorn ×1.00 → Void ×2.00, nine equal steps (1 + (1/9) × (grade# − 1))Inherited from the egg. Creatures are not upgraded with resources — they are bred and hatched, so this lever is raised by breeding for a better egg, and above Legendary it is a breeding pursuit outright. See Breeding.

The combined surface therefore runs ×1.00 at S1/Worn to ×2.50 at S3/Void. Nothing is ever below ×1.00 — a soul is never a penalty, only a bonus that grows.

Every item surface states its tier

LIVE on release since 2026-08-08 (0.42). An item’s tier is the single largest term in its stat base (coefficient × T × GF), and until now it was readable only by opening the item or reading a meta string. Every surface that draws an item now carries a tier plaque: a Roman numeral in the top-right corner of the item’s own box, on a near-black plate bordered in the item’s grade colour.

Five prop sites feed one plaque, so there is exactly one definition of what it says:

Mechanics. hasTier is !!it.tier, so an item with no tier — creatures, eggs, currency, an empty equipment slot — renders nothing at all rather than a zero or a blank plate. tierRoman is romanN(tier), the same converter that names the chronicle chapters; there is no second numeral table. The plaque is pointer-events:none on every surface, so it can never eat a click meant for the tile beneath it. Reward rows are the one place the tier is not carried on the row object: the drop code writes it into the sub string (Rare Weapon T7), so the mapper reads x.tier where it exists and otherwise parses /\bT(\d+)\b/ out of sub — no reward push site had to be touched.

Why this took three attempts. invGridProps had exposed a tierBadge prop since the inventory was written and no markup ever bound it. Earlier sessions read the prop, saw it populated, and went hunting for an occlusion bug that did not exist. The lesson is the standing one: a prop is not a pixel. tools/probe_tierplaque.py measures geometry, computed style and viewport containment on the real client — BEFORE 4/7 with zero plaques on any of the three surfaces, AFTER 10/10. Two of its own assertions were wrong first and were fixed rather than relaxed: the corner test measured the leaf span against itself (dc-runtime wraps bound text in a static inner span, so the positioned box is the parent) and answered 0/0, which reads as a pass; and the plaque failed the legibility floor at 7.3 CSS px, which is why the type is 15–16 stage px and not the 11 px it started at.

Auto-optimize — four defects, and one behaviour that is not one

Build 0.47. The optimizer could lose an enchant scroll, could hand back a loadout weaker than the one it replaced, scored the incumbent on terms it denied every challenger, and could never report a socket row as unchanged. Each is separate; they are listed in the order they cost the player most.

1. DATA LOSS — optApply() destroyed enchant scrolls. equipItem() unfolds an arriving item's it.socks onto its new instance socket key. That is the arriver unfolds half of D ruling other #4, whose other half is the leaver: gems and runes are refunded as loose bag items, and only the scroll (ki >= 2) folds onto the bag record — see Socket Ownership. optApply() performed the leaver half and never performed the arriver half. So a scroll that folded onto a bag record when the optimizer unequipped a piece was destroyed the moment the optimizer equipped it again: a round trip through the one button lost it, silently, with no refusal and no message. This is the same class of defect as the class-switch bug that destroyed 41 of a player's stones — a third code path emptying a slot without obeying the contract.

2. The plan could end WORSE than it started. Measured on a real save under the ATK objective: 35,887 → 35,694, with every gear row reading “X → X” — not one item moved, only stones. The cause is that fillFor always rebuilt a host's sockets from a clean slate: the incumbent's existing arrangement was reproduced only by luck, and a slot served earlier could take stones a later slot was already wearing. optOpen then refuses a regressing plan with “ALREADY OPTIMAL!” while the next click builds a different plan — which is why the reporting player had to click several times before anything happened.

Fixed three ways, and the three are meant to be read together:

ChangeMechanic
KEEP-WHAT-YOU-WEAR is a candidateThe incumbent arrangement is entered as a candidate and evaluated first. With the existing strict-greater-than acceptance test, every slot then either holds or strictly improves, so the pass is monotone BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by luck. A regression is no longer possible to build, so it is no longer something the refusal has to catch.
Slots are served biggest-host-base-firstA rune is a percentage OF ITS HOST, so the host with the biggest base must pick first — this is the rearrangement inequality, not a heuristic. statFits keeps each stat's hosts in their own relative order, so one pass serves every stat without a per-stat sweep.
A global re-deal with hosts finalOnce the hosts are settled, the whole loadout is re-dealt under several slot orders and the best kept, with the sweep's own result installed as the baseline so the re-deal cannot lose to it.

For a future session: a SINGLE-HOST release-and-refill is a no-op. fillFor is deterministic given the free pool, so releasing one host's stones and refilling that host returns exactly what was there. Only a global re-deal can move a stone between hosts. Anyone attempting to improve this by iterating one slot at a time will measure no change and conclude wrongly that the pool is already optimal.

Results on the same real save: ATK 35,887 → 36,160 where it had been losing 193; Overall Power +440; DEF +124; HP +2,742. Plan build time ~142 ms.

KNOWN REMAINING LIMIT, recorded honestly. Repeated clicks still find a little more: 36,160 → 36,452 over four runs. The residual is a genuine host-by-stone assignment problem across duplicate item instances — not a bug in the three changes above. Closing it requires restructuring the plan's bag accounting, which is what consume rides on, so it is a deliberate non-goal for 0.47 rather than an oversight.

3. The incumbent was scored WITH its enchant scroll and every challenger WITHOUT one. The socket strip removes only ki 0 and 1, so an incumbent kept its ki >= 2 scroll through scoring while a challenger — arriving with its scroll folded onto its bag record — was measured bare. A challenger's own ki >= 2 scroll is now folded in when it is scored. It cannot collide with the fill, which assigns ki 0 and 1 only, so the two never address the same socket key.

4. sameFill could never be true, so every socket row reported “changed” forever. It compared the incumbent's chips using an identity prefix with no |@gi instance suffix, although socket keys have been instance-keyed since bug #8. With duplicated items equipped — two Signets and four Fangs on the reporting save — the prefix matched 80 keys where 24 belonged to the slot, so the comparison could not come out equal on any run. The rows were not lying about a change they had made; they were answering a question that had been asked wrongly.

NOT A BUG — record this so it is not reported again: a T4 helmet outranking a T10 helmet is CORRECT. Socket capacity is GRADE-driven, via SLOTG — Void is 3/3/3, Rare is 1/1/1 — so a T4 Void piece carries six stones against a T10 Rare's two, and genuinely wins. Tier is not an input to the optimizer's decision, by design. It scores by real computed stat contribution through the buildItem / itemBase / itemStat chain and the game's own charMetric; the tier is already inside those numbers where it belongs, and adding it as a separate preference would double-count it.

The item census was refusing honest commits — report #175

Status: LIVE on release since 2026-09-02 (0.67). “Set auto-optimize to HP. Gear list pop up described the changes and showed that changes would be made. Changes are cancelled every time.” Six [auto] rows carried the same failure from four different accounts (#191, #206, #209, #243, #244, #259).

The plan was right and the commit was thrown away. optApply takes an item census before and after and refuses to commit if the totals differ by one — the guard written after the 0.47 scroll-destruction bug, and it is a good guard. But a stone has TWO homes, and the census read them differently:

  • Socketed on a worn instance, it is counted from sockOv with its category derived from the socket index in the key (0 Gem, 1 Rune, 2 Scroll).
  • Folded into a displaced piece’s socks when that piece goes back to the bag, it was counted straight off the record — and a folded record carries no cat field.

So one scroll was Scroll|Fated Scroll|10|Rare in a socket and |Fated Scroll|10|Rare in a fold. Any plan that moved a scroll between the two read as one item destroyed and a different one created, and the whole apply was abandoned. All six live refusals are scrolls, and all six balance exactly once the empty prefix is ignored — nothing was ever lost or created. Scrolls alone, because gems and runes are refunded to the bag as their own rows and those rows carry a category; a scroll is replace-only and travels inside the item it is socketed in.

The fix is one derivation shared by both homes — the fold now reads its category from the socks entry’s own ki, the same 0/1/2 the socket branch reads. The guard keeps its teeth: a commit that really destroys a scroll, and a commit that really duplicates one, are both still refused.

This also explains report #187 (“it did not replace … it did not add the socketables that were in my inventory”). Both fixes it could have been waiting on were already live when it was filed — the target/overall-power tie-break landed 2026-08-07 and the one-stone-twice fix landed 2026-08-13 — and an abandoned apply looks exactly like an optimiser that decided to do nothing. The reporter’s own save carries 20 scrolls socketed on worn gear and none in folds, so any plan that swaps one of those pieces out trips the mismatch. Confirmation on his save is owed before #187 is called closed.

Level requirements — what the build enforces (ON DEV since 2026-08-25, LIVE on release since 2026-09-01, 0.65)

The rule. Every equippable row in items.csv carries Level_Req = (tier−1)×10: T1 = 1, T2 = 10, … T9 = 80, T10 = 90 — gear, tags, gems, runes and scrolls alike. Until 2026-08-25 the only reader of that column coloured a label on the craft bench; a level-1 account could wear full T10. D's ruling the same day: the ACCOUNT level gates gear (the number on the HUD, playerLv, uncapped past 100) — not the class level, which keeps its own job at the 33/66 stage gates.

One test, five doors. lvlBlock(slot, item) answers the required level when it is not met and 0 otherwise, and everything reads it:

  • The equip picker still lists an over-level piece — greyed, not hidden, so the rule is learnable — with LV n across the card; clicking it toasts LEVEL n REQUIRED (tags: CREATURE LEVEL n REQUIRED) and equips nothing. equipItem refuses the same way as a backstop. Legal pieces (a T9 at level 88) equip exactly as before.
  • The socket picker greys an over-level gem, rune or scroll the same way and refuses on click before anything is consumed — a scroll never reaches the roll door.
  • The equipment screen: a piece worn above level is inert — buildItem returns 0 for it — and its slot's red block reads ILLEGAL / LV n (the second line used to be the fixed word UNEQUIP; it now names the level, and the creature-panel tag rows got the block for the first time — a tag never showed any illegal state before). An over-level stone or scroll shows the red ILLEGAL cell with a tip naming the level.
  • Auto-optimise never proposes an over-level gear candidate and never places an over-level stone from the pool; a worn over-level stone can only be kept (inert) or refunded to the bag like any other displaced stone.
  • The server's power figure (src/power.js, the leaderboard) applies the identical rule — parity gate green on real saves — so an inert piece is worth nothing on the board too.

Already worn above level follows the standing ruling on anything illegal (D, 2026-07-28): nothing is unequipped, unsocketed or moved on the player's behalf. The piece stays, contributes nothing, and comes alive by itself the moment the wearer reaches the level. Measured before the rule shipped (2026-08-25, all 18 alpha saves): 4 accounts wore character gear above their account level and 9 party creatures wore tags above their own level. D ordered all of them promoted to level 90 (accounts and creatures), which the ops record on Admin & backend covers; a wearer at 90 clears every requirement in the game, since nothing equippable asks for more.

Never judge on an empty codex. A row with no Level_Req (or a codex that has not landed yet) reads as no requirement, exactly like the stat-fit rule — an unreadable requirement never condemns a piece.
