BREEDING

Other Systems › Breeding

Breeding is a continuous pen, not a transaction. A pair is placed in a pen and the pen is toggled on; from then on it eats Ichor minute by minute and fills a progress bar. When the bar completes, an egg drops into the inventory and the pen keeps going. Ichor is the only limiter — it has no other use in the game.

The pen grid

Six pens, shown as a 2×3 grid. Pen 1 is unlocked from the start. Every other pen is bought with Ichor (D ruling, July 2026 — no DLC pens in this version). They unlock in order and the price climbs steeply from the third: the last two are long-haul goals, not purchases. Costs are authored in codex/pen_unlocks.csv and read by the client — never hardcoded.

What extra pens actually buy. Not throughput — Ichor income caps that regardless of pen count. Extra pens buy parallelism: several different pairs, types, and grade-ladders running at once, and no idle time while you swap partners. A player with six pens and a player with two pens hatch the same number of Void eggs per hour; the six-pen player is also climbing five other lines while they do it.

What decides an egg’s grade

The parents’ LEVEL does not matter. Their GRADE does, and nothing else does. A level 100 Worn pair and a level 1 Worn pair lay exactly the same eggs. Level is read for experience and for combat; it is never read anywhere on the breeding path.

What the server does, in order:

  1. Takes each parent’s grade from its own item ledger — never from the client’s note and never from the uploaded save.
  2. Floors the average of the two: P = max(1, floor((gradeIndex(♂) + gradeIndex(♀)) / 2)). An Epic × Worn pair is floor((5+1)/2) = 3 — an Uncommon pen. A mismatched pair always averages down, so two parents of the same grade is the efficient pairing.
  3. Uses that pen grade to pick one row of codex/egg_hatch_weights.csv. The egg’s grade is then random within that row — the row sets the odds, not the result. A Void pen lays a Void egg 78.1% of the time and can still lay a Worn one at 0.1%.

The same pen grade also sets the wait and the cost out of codex/breeding.csv. The grade is re-derived every time the pen is written, not remembered, so correcting a parent’s grade heals the pen without restarting the timer. Full odds table under Egg grade output; the rates are under Rates.

Choosing a pair

The pen has two sockets, ♂ and ♀. Clicking a socket opens a full-screen roster picker — it leaves the pen grid rather than crowding it, so the 2×3 grid stays intact.

Type scarcity is the real gate. Across the roster of 300, the median creature has 45 or 46 type-compatible species (45.5 exactly) and the tightest has 11. The four three-creature types — Celestial, Demon, Chimera, Aberration — are a single line each, so they can only breed within their own line. Breeding rewards a broad collection, not a strong one.

What the egg contains

The egg carries one of the two parents' lines, rolled 50/50 at the moment it is laid — never a third species. It hatches that line's Stage 1, like every other egg in the game. The shared type is the gate on which pairs may breed; it does not decide what comes out, so pairing a Thornback with a Dusthound returns a Bristle or a Whelp and nothing else. The egg's shell art follows the rolled species' first type, and its grade is rolled separately from the pair grade (below).

The line is fixed when the egg is laid, not when it hatches, so an egg sitting in the inventory already knows what it is.

Pair grade

Every rate in the system keys off one number: pair grade P.

P = floor( (grade of parent A + grade of parent B) / 2 )

Mismatched parents are punished by the floor: a Void paired with a Rare breeds as Abyssal (P = 7), not as Void. Climbing the grade ladder means lifting both parents.

Where the server gets the two grades. The start note names only the two parent ids. The server looks each one up in its own item ledger and reads the grade there — never from the note and never from the uploaded save — then floors the average exactly as above. Three rules follow: a ledgered parent whose grade is not one of the ten legal grades records nothing (the start is unverifiable and the egg still pays off the claim, flagged, like any other unverifiable start); a pair re-announced unchanged never restarts its wait, but its recorded grade is re-derived on every note, so a wrong record cannot outlive the next battle; and the recorded grade is what the claim uses for the wait, the weights and the settled cost. Gate: tools/probe_breedgrade.mjs 7/7 — it reads the recorded grade back through the claim door's remaining wait (Void 110, Abyssal 50, Common 18 minutes), which is the one place a sandbox can see it.

The loop

Toggle OFFDrain stops. Progress is retained, not lost.
Egg inventory fullDrain stops, the bar freezes at its current fill. Nothing is consumed and nothing is lost. Clear an egg and it resumes.
Ichor hits zeroSame as above: drain stops, bar freezes, pen resumes the moment Ichor arrives.
Removing a parentResets that pen's meter to zero. This is what stops a player filling a bar on a cheap Worn pair and swapping in two Voids at 99%.
ClockSession time only, and session means the tab is alive — not that it is in front of you. A pen advances on wall-clock elapsed time whenever the client is loaded, including while the window sits unfocused or behind another tab. It does not advance while the game is closed: no offline accrual, ever.

How the meter actually advances

  • The tick is a function of elapsed wall-clock time, not of frames drawn. Each tick credits prog += (minutes elapsed × speed) / Minutes_Per_Egg and spends Ichor_Per_Min × minutes elapsed.
  • It therefore does not matter how often the tick runs. A browser that throttles a background tab to one tick a minute credits that whole minute on the next call; the pen finishes at the same moment either way.
  • Partial ticks are honest: if there is not enough Ichor for the full interval the pen credits the fraction it could pay for and flags LOW FOOD. At zero Ichor it pauses without consuming anything.
  • A pen that reaches 100% with the soul bag full freezes at 100% and reports SOUL BAG FULL. Nothing is consumed and no egg is lost — it lays as soon as a slot exists.
  • speed is 1 on release: one game minute is one real minute. The ×60 accelerator exists only in development builds.

Ichor income

Ichor comes from combat, and scales with what you killed — level and rarity both. Per defeated enemy:

Ichor = round( 2 × (1 + enemyLevel / 20) × RarityMult )

No breed-and-dismantle loop. Yields are tuned against the hatch-weight matrix so that the expected dismantle value of an egg is always 27–30% of what the pen spent making it — at every rung of the ladder, including the up-rolls. Breeding for Ichor is always a loss; dismantling is a refund on a bad roll, never an engine.

Egg grade output

The pen does not need a new matrix. The pair grade P indexes the existing row in codex/egg_hatch_weights.csv — the same matrix that later rolls the hatchling's grade from the egg's grade. Two Void parents roll a Void egg 78.1% of the time (14% Primordial, 5% Divine, and a long tail below), and that Void egg then rolls a Void creature 78.1% of the time: ~61% Void end-to-end, with a real chance of falling short at either step. The Hatchery grade-boost upgrade shifts the second roll upward.

Throughput ceiling

Ichor income — not pen count — is the hard cap on egg production.

A single Void pen alone eats half of a maxed player's entire Ichor income. That is the intended shape: the top of the ladder is slow by construction, and no amount of pens buys past it.

Where breeding happens — anchor and claim

The server is called once, at the end. Starting a pen costs no request of its own and no tick is ever reported. The client attaches two fields — the pen index and its grade — to whatever call it was already making, and the server anchors the pen. POST /api/breed/claim is the only breeding request in the system.

StepWhat happens
StartThe client rides {p, g, s} on an existing request — pen, grade, and the moment it started. Because the note is queued, the pen really did start before the server heard about it, so that time is credited rather than discarded — but it is clamped: start = clamp(claimed, floor = last contact, ceiling = now). The floor falls out of the offline rule: nothing happens offline, so a pen cannot have started before the last moment the account was talking to the server. A false claim is therefore worth at most the gap since the client's previous call, which the client's own traffic already bounds, and a backdate to 1970 buys nothing at all. The floor is read from writes the account already makes (the battle chain's last flush, the stored save's timestamp), so proving last contact costs no extra write. With no claim, or a nonsense one, the anchor falls back to now().
RunningNothing. No tick, no heartbeat, no request. The pen is a row with a grade and a timestamp, and it survives a worker restart because it lives in the account's SQL.
Re-announcedIgnored. A re-sent note does not restart a running pen — otherwise a client could re-announce forever and hold an egg at 99%.
ClaimThe server compares elapsed time against Minutes_Per_Egg for that grade. Early, it answers the remaining wait and grants nothing. Ready, it returns the egg with the authored Ichor_Per_Egg and Egg_Dismantle_Ichor, and consumes the pen so one wait can never pay twice.
Every number comes from breeding.csv — minutes per egg, ichor per minute, ichor per egg, dismantle value. Nothing is hardcoded, and a grade the file does not author grants nothing at all rather than falling back to a default.

The egg's grade and its line are the server's

LIVE on release since 2026-08-08 (0.42). Server-side rolls items 2 and 3, and they are one ask, because they are one decision made at one instant. Split into two calls, a client could keep a grade it liked and re-ask for the line.

D's breeding ruling is unchanged: “Server only needs to be called when breeding ends for the result. Starting breeding and the ticks can be queued.” So the per-second tick still runs locally and still meters Ichor in whole units. Only the laying waits.

A due egg is a paid entitlement, and the pen stops

Breeding charges as it goes, so by the time progress reaches 1 the Ichor is already spent. The egg cannot simply be dropped if the server is unreachable, and it must not be laid blind either. So the pen banks due and stops: it spends no more Ichor and accrues no second entitlement. That is what keeps this out of outbox territory — a stopped pen credits nothing, and what is owed is one egg, not a growing queue. It reuses the rule breeding already has: a full soul bag pauses a pen, it never discards.

layEgg() is idempotent against its own entitlement, so an answer arriving twice cannot lay twice, and it re-checks the soul cap — the bag can fill between the entitlement being banked and the answer landing.

Adam and Eve — the soul-bag pair keeper (reports #99 + #100)

Status: on release. An option in the soul-bag dismantle menu, offered in the SOUL BAG ONLY because it is meaningless where there is no line and no gender. It keeps the highest-grade MALE and the highest-grade FEMALE of every advancement line and dismantles the rest. Two keepers per line, not one — a breeding pair needs both, which is the whole point of the name.

What it refuses to touch, each a deliberate refusal rather than an omission:

  • a creature whose gender is not known. The gender helper defaults to male, and that default must never decide a dismantle — unknown is its own bucket and everything in it is kept. Wrong in the player's favour is the only acceptable direction here.
  • a creature whose line cannot be resolved from creatures.csv. No line, no comparison, no dismantle.
  • anything in a breeding pen, which is in use.
  • class souls, eggs and everything the shared bulk-salvage rules already exclude — it runs through those rules rather than restating them, so every existing exclusion holds for free.

Ties are broken by level, then by stage, and the confirmation window says so. A tie means the two are interchangeable, but only one survives, so the choice is made on the numbers rather than on bag order.

The confirmation window explains what it does, which is part of the spec rather than a nicety: it names the rule, how many survive across how many lines, how many are being dismantled, and every creature it is leaving alone and why. Without that last part a player reads “keeps the best of each line”, finds four Phantoms still in the bag, and files it as a bug.

Measured on a real account before shipping: 87 creatures down to 47, exactly the 40 on the kill list, no creature dismantled while a worse one of its own line and gender survived, a second run finding nothing left to do, and the item census changing creature rows only. Client: adamEveSplit(), dismTargets mode adameve, dismAdamEve in play/app.js.
