PREMIUM SHOP

Server-authoritative store for Wraithgold. Prices and durations are authored in codex/premium_shop.csv — never in code.

What is for sale

SKUNameCostDurationEffect

SHOP-002Bait10 Wraithgold30 daysHalves the wild respawn interval; stacks with Incense

The authored respawner gives one spawn per 60 seconds while below the cap of ten. Incense halves that interval to 30 seconds. Bait multiplies whatever interval is running by 0.5: 30 seconds alone, 15 seconds with Incense (interval = (60 s − incense 30 s) × bait 0.5, tables.json spawn_budget). A timed boon bought again adds 30 days. Auto PVP Attacks is sold by the same door; the money is spent in the player's object and the ten tickets are granted in the world object on the purchase's own idem, so a retry ends at exactly +10 once. The pool cap is unchanged — the cap is how many creatures can be waiting, not how fast they arrive.

The boon starts when it is paid for and never reaches backwards. Credits accrue lazily — the row holds a balance and the instant it was last touched, and the accrual splits that window at the boon boundary, so time before the purchase settles at 60 seconds and time after it at 30. A purchase therefore settles the row first: it banks what the old rate earned, stamps the instant, and only then hangs the new expiry.

Until 2026-08-07 the purchase wrote the expiry alone and left the instant at the last spawn spent, so the whole already-elapsed window was re-accrued at the boosted rate — buying after three idle minutes handed the player six spawns for three minutes of ordinary waiting. Bounded by the cap of ten, and always in the player's favour, but paid time that was never paid for.

How a purchase works

The client names a SKU and an idempotency key. It never sends a price. The worker resolves the authored row and the player's own object performs the balance check, the debit and the grant inside a single transaction. A repeated key returns the original answer rather than charging twice.

RuleBehaviour
Insufficient balanceRefused with 402. A balance never goes negative on a purchase.
Repeat purchase while activeExtends the expiry by another 30 days. Time already paid for is never burned.
Unauthored SKURefused. Only rows in the CSV are for sale.
Catalogue unreachableRefuses to sell rather than inventing a price.
ExpiryComputed server-side from the grant. A save rollback can neither lose nor extend it.

The wallet ledger

Wraithgold is held in an append-only ledger. The balance is the sum of its entries; there is no authoritative balance stored anywhere else. Every entry carries its provenance, so paid, granted and promotional units stay distinguishable — that distinction cannot be reconstructed once units mix.

A progress wipe does not touch it. Resetting a realm clears saves and progress; currency that was paid for survives.

Item grants

Items owed to a player — compensation, purchases, promotions — are minted server-side and stay pending until the client takes delivery and records the grant in its own save. If a save is rolled back, the record rolls back with it and the grant becomes owed again, so a restore can never silently swallow an item. A grant with nowhere to go waits rather than being destroyed.

The incense prompt (P7, shipped 2026-08-03)

When a floor's spawns are exhausted and no boon is running, the floor offers BURN INCENSE TO GET MORE SPAWNS. incPromptOn and onIncensePrompt had been computed and inert in app.js with no markup at all — the block was removed and never rebuilt. Six candidate causes were ruled out across earlier attempts (the scene, the condition, a missing spawnSb, wiped markup, a missing hint-placeholder-val, and placement) without finding one, because the block was not in the document to begin with. It is deliberately a sibling of the drought gate rather than nested inside it: nesting in the drought pill is the placement that was already tried and emitted nothing.

It shows only when the pool is empty and no Incense boon is active, and hides the moment one is burned. Probe: tools/probe_deadprops.py (8/8), which asserts the condition is true before believing the render.

Incense — the player can see it burning (the pill described here became an icon tile on 2026-09-06 — next section)

Incense doubles wild-spawn refill for 30 days. The expiry has always been server-side (spawn_budget.boon_until) and /api/floor/enter has always returned it to the client as inc, which the client stored as spawnInc — and nothing ever rendered it, so a player who had paid for Incense had no way to tell how much was left. The floor screen now shows a pill reading INCENSE · 4d 23h, under the MENU button. Coarse by design: the boon runs 30 days, so days and hours are the useful unit.

The pill is a floor-scene element: incLeftOn requires the floor phase and that no panel is open, because panels are overlays and the phase stays floor underneath every one of them.

Two delivery holes closed on 2026-08-07. The buy answer now returns the expiry and the spawn budget alongside the balance, so Incense bought while standing on a floor takes effect on screen immediately instead of waiting for the next /api/floor/enter — previously the shop said it was active and the floor showed nothing. And incPump() arms a single timeout on the boon's own expiry, so the pill and the upsell prompt stop reading a boon that has ended on an idle floor; the one-second drought tick only runs while the pool is starved and could not serve this.

Placement was measured, not chosen. The obvious spot — centred under the floor title — is covered by the floor header bar, which is z-index 80 with an opaque background. A pill placed there is present in the DOM with every computed style reading visible, and invisible on screen. The centre lower down belongs to the incense prompt (top:12%), which can be showing at the same time. Under MENU is clear, and z-index 105 clears the bar. Verify this class of change with document.elementFromPoint() at the element's own centre, never by reading its text.

Panel size and offer layout

Since #1050 (2026-10-01) the shop is a full-stage screen on the PvP Arena’s shape, not a window: a 65px header (the title on the banner, the 64px Wraithgold coin and balance, a cross), then the Shadow Trader standing in a 560 stage px column on the left and the catalogue in the painted 9-slice frame on the right, the frame’s inner column the stage’s remaining height with overflow:auto as the safety valve. The note is pinned to the bottom of the frame so a short catalogue does not leave it floating. Before that it was a min(1320px,94%) × 94% window with 24px 36px of padding (#958, 2026-09-17 — and min(1180px,94%) at 86% before the Shadow Trader tab turned out not to fit), and a fixed 520px column before 2026-08-05.

The offers are a LIST, one row per sku (display:flex; flex-direction:column; gap:12px). They were briefly a responsive grid (auto-fill, minmax(360px,1fr)); that is reversed as of 2026-08-05. The grid capped every card at roughly 360 stage px, and the reason that matters is the stage: the whole UI is a fixed 1920×1080 box scaled by fitScale = min(innerWidth/1920, innerHeight/1080), which on a phone is about 0.36. Authored sizes are therefore stage units, and what a player sees is the size × fitScale. Measured on a 690×674 viewport, the old 14px offer name drew at 5.03 CSS px and the buy control stood 12 CSS px tall — below any usable tap target.

Dismissal

The panel closes three ways: the CLOSE control, and — since 2026-08-05 — a tap anywhere on the backdrop. The backdrop carries onCloseShop and the window itself carries onStopProp, so a tap inside never bubbles out and closes it. This is the same pattern the shop backdrop simply carried no handler at all, so a tap outside did nothing. Verified with document.elementFromPoint() at a point derived from the backdrop's rect minus the window's — note that on a phone the stage is letterboxed, so the top-left of the SCREEN is page background and is not outside the window.
