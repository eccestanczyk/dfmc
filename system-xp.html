XP & LEVELING

Other Systems › XP & Leveling

Two-phase leveling built for an autoplay climb: a gentle journey to level 66 that opens the whole tower fast, then a steep geometric endgame that carries hundreds of hours.

The curve is deliberately back-loaded: about 75% of all leveling time sits in levels 90–100, and 50% in the last five levels. Total to max: ~20,300 battles ≈ 680 h. Reaching level 67 — where wild Stage 3 creatures appear — takes only ~885 battles (~30 h).

“On-level” is a floor, not a forecast — and live accounts do not sit on it. Those two columns assume the character is standing on the floor whose number matches their level. That is the case where the level-gap penalty is zero, so it is the cheapest the climb can possibly be. It is not what the tower actually looks like. Measured against all 11 closed-alpha accounts (live saves, floors 1–99): the class level runs +1 to +19 levels above the floor through the early and middle tower — a median of about +7 — and then falls 17–19 levels behind at floors 97–99, where the requirement curve outruns the climb. Exactly two of eleven sit inside the zero-penalty band. Treat the two on-level columns as a lower bound and multiply by the table below.

What actually sets the number. Per-kill XP is indexed by the enemy’s level, so XP per battle is set by the floor you farm and never by your own level — out-levelling a floor earns you nothing extra, it only costs. Three things are measured rather than assumed:

  • 3.00 kills per battle, exactly — on 9 of 9 alpha accounts that have fought one (_ledK / _ledB from the server's own ledger). The assumption behind every battle count on this page.
  • A wild slot is not stamped at the floor's level. buildSpawn() rolls an offset of −3…+3 at 3/7/12/56/12/7/3%, clamped 1–100, and the whole spread sits inside the grace band, so on-level really is penalty-free on every roll. Averaging over it rather than reading the floor's own row moves the number by under 0.35% above level 4 — one battle in 20,346 across the climb.
  • The penalty is visible in the ledger. Account XP is raw, class XP is penalised, so _ledC / _ledX is the retention the account actually got. On the reference account — class 31 on floor 23, a gap of +8 — the formula predicts 0.796 and the ledger reads 0.807.

Player level (uncapped)

Separate from creature levels, class levels, and character level. The player level is an account-wide marker of total time in the tower — it is purely cosmetic today and grants nothing: no stats, no unlocks, no gear requirements. It exists so that the endgame keeps counting, and so that a future system has a natural place to hook into.

PropertyRule
XP sourceEvery point of XP the account earns, from every battle, in every realm. Player XP is never split — it is the raw total.
Curve, levels 1–100Identical to the creature / class curve: 10 × L^1.75, then 234 × L × 1.16^(L−67).
Curve, levels 101+Flat. Every level past 100 costs a fixed PLAYER_FLAT_XP = 639,723 XP — the level-90 requirement, held constant. No cap; the counter never stops. Kept as a single tunable constant rather than derived from the curve, so the vanity pace can be retuned without touching the leveling model.
Hardcore deathPlayer level is not lost. Characters, creatures, items, and class XP are wiped; the player level is the one number that survives, because it measures the account, not the run.
GrantsNothing. Deliberately inert.

Pacing, past 100: at level-100 enemies (10 × 100 = 1,000 XP per kill) each player level is ~640 battles ≈ 21 h of autoplay — roughly one level a fortnight for a steady player, so the counter visibly ticks over instead of freezing. Reaching player level 100 itself takes ~21.6 M XP; every level after is a flat ~640 k grind, climbing linearly forever instead of exploding. Continuing the level-100 requirement (3.1 M, ~105 h/level) was rejected as too slow to feel alive.

Who receives XP

  • End-of-battle XP goes to every creature in the party and the avatar, not only the participants.
  • Dying in battle does not forfeit XP — a fainted creature or downed avatar still receives its full share.
  • Each recipient's XP is then scaled by the level gap to the defeated enemy. See Level Penalty for the full curve.
Hardcore implication: death wipes a character to level 1. The relevel to 67 is ~30 h of autoplay, but a death at level 95+ costs a dedicated player weeks — high stakes by design. See Death & Realms.

Class XP — who computes it

The server, since 2026-08-01. Class XP is the avatar's share of the same battle, and it is derived from the same authored tables the account total comes from — the client is not asked and is not believed.

QuantityRule
Account XP (player level)The raw sum of XP_Kill_<tier> over the kills the server derived from its own seeded slot. No penalty is applied — player XP is never split
Class XPThe same kills, each scaled by the level-gap penalty between the avatar's class level and that enemy: round(base × (1 − penalty(enemyLevel − classLevel))). The class level is read once, before the battle
Class levelDerived from the ledger's own class total against XP_Cumulative in XPCurve.csv — the server needs nothing from the client to know it
CeilingClasses cap at level 100, so 18,464,128 (the cumulative at 100) is an absolute ceiling on class XP. Below that, a save may only claim what the server has credited since the previous accepted save

Two consequences fall straight out of the definitions. Class XP can never exceed account XP, because the penalty is never negative — an account where it does has a starved account total, not a rich class total. And at a level gap inside the grace window the penalty is zero, so the two totals are identical; that is the sharpest available check that both are being computed from the same table.

What this replaces. Until this landed the ledger held account XP, Ichor, kills and battles — and nothing at all for class XP. The save door capped it only on a newborn account, then merged it grow-only forever after, so on any established account a crafted save could set class XP to any number and keep it permanently. Class advancement was the last progression system still authored entirely by the client.

Grandfathering. The class ledger starts counting from the day it shipped while every live account's class XP is historical, so the ceiling is a delta: each accepted save is stamped with the server's class total at that moment, and the next save may claim what was credited since, plus the standard grace. An account with no stamp yet keeps what it holds. This is the same shape as the kill and battle counters, and for the same reason — a flat total would have confiscated real progress on the first save.

Who credits a battle's XP — nobody but the server

ON DEV, not yet on release. Owner ruling, standing: no client-credited battle rewards. Account XP, class XP and Ichor are not moved when a battle ends. /api/battle answers with the figures the server derived from its own kill list, and those are the only numbers that ever reach the account or the screen.

What this replaced, stated precisely, because the end state was already correct. Since 2026-08-03 the client credited its own arithmetic and then reconciled to the server's number as a delta when the response landed — so the stored total was already the server's. What was wrong was the transient: for the 67–256 ms until the response arrived, the player's totals held a figure the server had never agreed to, and an uncredited battle (rate cap, spawn budget) had to be adjusted back down to zero afterwards. The local credit is now zero, which makes that same delta reconciliation an absolute apply with no second code path.

  • Timing is not the obstacle and was measured, not assumed. The victory bar stages at 80 / 1000 / 1070 ms while a live round trip is 67–256 ms, so the server's figure lands an order of magnitude inside the fill animation. The bar simply grows to it.
  • The victory class row is rebuilt from the server's number too. Account XP is rendered nowhere on the victory screen, so the class row is the one battle figure a player actually reads; leaving it on the client's estimate would have kept showing a number the server never agreed to. The row's XP text, both percentages and the level-up marker are recomputed from the same level function the row was built with, so bar and label can never disagree.
  • Creature XP is untouched by this. The server does not compute per-creature XP, so crXp is still client-side. It is a separate question from the account ledger.
  • An uncredited or unanswered battle now credits nothing at all, which is the ruling working: the server credited nothing, so the player earns nothing. A battle cannot start without connectivity in the first place — the battle-entry gate awaits a save flush and refuses the fight if it does not land.

Why it matters beyond correctness. The two authorities disagreeing by design is what made xp_over_ledger fire on every live account — hundreds of times each, on four of four accounts measured. A guard that fires on every honest player describes the system, not the players, and a real offender would have been invisible inside it. With one authority the flag becomes a signal again.

Advancement is asked for, not given

ON DEV, not yet on release. A creature, a socketed soul or the class still becomes eligible to advance the same way — by passing its line's advance level with a further stage to reach. What changed is who starts it. A victory no longer interrupts itself to play the scene. The win resolves, the rewards land, and the floor returns.

The advancement waits on the slot that earned it. An upward arrow sits on that slot's icon — the teammate's portrait, or the equipment slot holding the soul — and clicking it plays the advancement scene. Inspecting a creature and clicking its slot is how a player reaches it.

  • The arrow and the scene are one call. The marker is computed from the same function the scene uses, so an arrow can never appear on a slot the scene will not go to.
  • One at a time, in order — party creature, then socketed soul, then class. That was always the rule; it is simply now visible. A second eligible creature is marked once the first has been played.
  • Nothing expires. Eligibility is read from the creature's level, not from an edge, so an advancement left unclaimed stays claimable across battles, floors and reloads.

A creature does not state its tier

ON DEV, not yet on release. Rarity still exists and still governs everything it always did — spawn tables, encounter weights, art, the codex. What it no longer does is announce itself. Owner ruling: players work it out by meeting a creature more or less often.

Removed from every in-game surface: the badge on the creature card, the badge on the creature sheet, the badges in the hatch and codex lists, and the tier baked into a creature's subtitle. The abbreviation function still exists and is still correct — nothing renders it.

The tier colour stays (owner ruling). A card still reads at a glance without naming what it is. Item grades are a separate system and are untouched.
