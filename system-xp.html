Other Systems › XP & Leveling

Two-phase leveling built for an autoplay climb: a gentle journey to level 66 that opens the whole tower fast, then a steep geometric endgame that carries hundreds of hours.

The two phases are continuous at level 67. Per-kill XP is not flat in the enemy's level — it is the authored table — but a whole battle tracks 10 × L closely enough that the battle counts follow the requirement curve almost exactly.

XP curve

Log scale, plotted from the game’s XP table. Requirement for the next level and cumulative XP to reach the level. The knee at 67 is where 10 × L^1.75 hands off to a geometric phase whose per-level growth ramps smoothly from +2.7% (the polynomial's own rate at 66) to +16% at level 99 — no step anywhere. Levels 90–100 are 60% of the total.

Battles curve

The same curve divided by what a battle actually pays — battles spent at each level and cumulative battles to reach it, at the level-appropriate floor's own rarity mix over the −3…+3 spawn spread. Log scale. Level 100 has no bar of its own: it is the finish line, about 3,720 battles out (see Milestones).

Level 100 is the cap for creatures and classes; the player level continues at a flat 354,245 XP per level. Battle counts assume level-appropriate enemies, where the level-gap penalty is zero — see Level Penalty.

Player level (uncapped)

Separate from creature levels and class levels. The player (account) level is an account-wide marker of total time in the tower. It grants no stats and no unlocks; its one job is to gate character gear: gear of tier N needs account level (N−1)×10 (tier 1 needs level 1, tier 10 needs 90; details on Equipment). Creature tags are gated by the creature's own level, never by this one.

Who receives XP

  • Your account (player level) gets the battle’s full XP: the sum of its 3 kills, never reduced.
  • Your class, both party creatures and every socketed soul get the same kills, each scaled by the level gap between that recipient and the enemy (Level Penalty). A soul levels while it sits in its socket.
  • Each class line has its own level. A battle pays the class you fought as. Classes and creatures cap at level 100.
  • Dying in battle does not forfeit XP — a fainted creature or downed avatar still receives its full share.
  • The server credits every battle. The XP and Ichor on the victory screen are the server’s figures; a battle the server does not credit pays nothing.

Advancement

A creature, a socketed soul or your class becomes eligible to advance by passing its line’s advance level with a further stage to reach. A victory never interrupts itself to play the scene: the win resolves, the rewards land, and the floor returns.

  • The arrow. An upward arrow appears dead centre on the record that earned it — the party creature’s card, the equipped soul’s tile, the creature’s tile in the soul bag, or the class portrait. Click it to play the advancement scene; the arrow goes as soon as you click.
  • One at a time, in order — party creature, then socketed soul, then class. The next eligible record is marked once the first has played.
  • Nothing expires. Eligibility is read from the level, so an advancement left unclaimed stays claimable across battles, floors and reloads.
  • During a battle it waits. Clicking an arrow mid-fight shows ADVANCEMENT WILL PLAY AFTER THE BATTLE; it plays once you are back on the floor or in the boss den, several in a row as separate scenes.

Creatures do not state their tier

Rarity still governs spawn tables, encounter weights, art and the codex, but no in-game screen names it: you learn it by meeting a creature more or less often. The tier colour stays, so a card still reads at a glance. Item grades are a separate system.

Aptitude is a shape

A creature’s base attributes are drawn as a three-axis radar: ATK at the top, DEF lower right, SPD lower left, rings at 25/50/75/100%, values out of ten. HP is not charted. The shape reads as what a creature is for, and it matches the class pages’ charts.

Move upgrades follow the stages

Every move exists in three versions, and a creature moves up through them by level:

  • V1 is learned on the way to the second stage.
  • V2 lands inside the second-stage window — between the stage-2 and stage-3 advances.
  • V3 lands after the stage-3 advance, so a creature does not wield its final form’s moves before reaching that form. Three lines run one move ahead of this: Jackanape, Goliath and Charnelmass each upgrade their third move a few levels before the matching advance.
  • Everything is fully upgraded by level 90.
  • Each line has its own shape, so every level from 2 to 90 brings some creature a new move or an upgrade.
