THE SHADOW TRADER

Other Systems › The Shadow Trader

A tab of the Premium Shop, open all week (#770, D 2026-09-07 — LIVE on release since 2026-09-08 (0.71).; weekend-only until then). Every week the trader takes every currency except Wraithgold and hands back one currency — a different one each week — at a rate that is computed from how long each currency takes to earn, never authored by hand. Status: designed and BUILT 2026-08-25 (D, report #475) — rate model, quote and trade doors, the weekend tab. ON DEV; release at D's word.

The rules (D, 2026-08-25)

RuleWhat it means
All week (#770)The tab is there every day. The weekly cadence is the rotation alone: what he pays in turns over at Monday 00:00 UTC (the same clock as the daily reset), and the tab says what next week pays in. Until 0.71 promotes, release still runs the Friday-noon-to-Monday-noon JST window (#628).
One currency a weekEach week has one target: the currency he pays out. Week 0 (the week of Monday 2026-08-24) is Ichor, then Scrap, Dust, Essence, Ink, and round again. He takes every other currency — Scrap, Ichor, Dust, Essence, Ink — and never Wraithgold, in or out.
Rates from acquisition timeThe price of one unit of the week's currency, paid in another, is the ratio of how fast the two are earned, times the trader's cut. Earned means: what a battle yields in expectation, through the same drop and dismantle tables the game runs on. See the model below.
Self-adjustingThere is no rate table anywhere. The rate is derived at request time from drop_chances_by_floor.csv, drop_grade_weights.csv, drop_rates.csv, encounter_weights.csv, floors.csv, items.csv, dismantle.csv, breeding.csv and the Ichor-per-kill formula. Raise or lower any of them and the rates follow on the next request — a balance change can never leave the trader out of step.
One rate for everyoneD, 2026-08-25: “The shadow trader cannot have a dynamic per account rate. It needs to be the same for everyone, and based off endgame playtime.” Every rate is computed at one reference floor, TRADER_REFERENCE_FLOOR (the endgame), never at the account's own floor. The two candidate floors are tabled below — 99 and 119, the wild floors; 100 and 120 are boss floors with no wild encounters, and the model prices a boss floor at the wild floor below it, so a table headed “floor 100” was floor 99's numbers (D caught the label 2026-08-25). D picked 119 (“119 as a base for the shadow trader”, 2026-08-25): TRADER_REFERENCE_FLOOR = 119.
The cutTRADER_PREMIUM = 0.25: you pay time-parity × 1.25, i.e. you get back 80% of what the same time would have earned you directly. One constant, in src/trader.js; D's to tune.
Whole numbers, no remainderA trade is quoted as pay P of A, receive G of B with both whole. P is exactly what G costs (rounded up to the unit); a request too small to buy one unit is refused, never partially taken.
Server-authoritativeThe client sends lines [{from, amount}] and an idem key, nothing else. The Worker prices every line off the tables; the player's object replays on the key, enforces a 30-second per-account cooldown, validates every line against the stored wallet (a short line refuses with what you hold and what it needs), then writes the exchange into the stored save itself (version bumped) and answers the new balances and version — the client only adopts. The client also saves before asking, so income still only in memory is never overwritten by the adopted wallet. The door sits in the wallet rate class on top of the cooldown.
The tab (D's UX)A slider per other currency, from 0 to the balance at the moment the shop opened. Above each, the rate reads 1 : x at rest and y : x as the slider moves (y paid, x received). ACQUIRING <total> <currency> at the very top. CANCEL / CONFIRM at the bottom; CONFIRM opens a confirmation window listing PAY y A → GET x B per line and the total; CONFIRM TRADE sends one request with every line. After a trade both buttons count down 30 s. Errors are plain sentences. The whole tab fits the panel without a scrollbar (#958, D 2026-09-17) — and because the stage is fixed at 1920×1080, that is a property of the stage geometry, true at every viewport rather than at a lucky one. Beautifying the tab is a backlog item.

The model — what one battle yields at floor F

Battles per hour cancel out of every ratio, so “acquisition time” reduces to expected units per battle. One battle at floor F, as the worker runs it:

  • Three kills, each of a rarity drawn from encounter_weights.csv for F.
  • Ichor per kill = round(2 × (1 + min(100, F)/100) × mult[rarity]) — the battle credit's own formula since #772 (2026-09-10), mult from tables.json food_rarity_mult: C 1 / UC 1.1 / R 1.2 / SR 1.35 / SSR 1.5.
  • Three lanes per kill — gear, enhancement, egg — each landing with Chance_<rarity> from drop_chances_by_floor.csv.
  • A gear drop is uniform over the items.csv bases of the floor's tier among Weapon / Armour / Accessory / Tag, graded by the floor's drop_grade_weights band, and dismantles per dismantle.csv (Gear rows; a Tag also returns Essence).
  • An enhancement drop is an even Gem / Rune / Scroll split, graded one band behind (drop_rates.csv Enhancement_Grade_Rule), and dismantles to Dust / Essence / Ink.
  • An egg drop is graded by the band clamped at the wild-egg cap and dismantles to breeding.csv Egg_Dismantle_Ichor.
  • Not modelled, on purpose: bosses, first-win bonuses, keys, shop drops — they are not the steady state a rate should describe.

Then, for the week's target B and any other currency A: rate(A→B) = perBattle(A) ÷ perBattle(B) × (1 + premium), in units of A per one B.

The reference floor — 99 and 119, tabled for D (codex as of 2026-09-30, #772 Ichor curve, one battle every 15 s)

An Ichor week at the reference floor 119: 1,000 Scrap buys 559 Ichor; 10 Dust buys 98 Ichor (time parity would be 12.3 Ichor per Dust; 9.8 after the cut). A Dust week at 119: one Dust costs about 15.4 Ichor, or 22 Scrap.

A note the model surfaced (not changed): drop_rates.csv says each lane lands “exactly 10% per battle (3 kills)”, but the authored per-kill chances, mix-weighted on the encounter weights, come to ~6.7% per kill — about 19% per battle per lane, roughly twice the stated figure. The trader's ratios are unaffected (every drop-fed currency scales together), but the sentence and the table disagree; D's call which is right.
