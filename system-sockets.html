SOCKET OWNERSHIP

Other Systems › Socket Ownership

What happens to a gem, a rune or an enchant scroll in a piece of gear or a creature tag, and what happens to it when the piece leaves its slot. Companion page: Equipment & Auto-Optimize.

Sockets at a glance

A piece of gear or a creature tag can carry three kinds of socketed item. How many of each it holds depends on its grade, never its tier: a Worn piece has no sockets, a Rare piece holds 1 gem, 1 rune and 1 scroll, a Void piece 3 / 3 / 3 (the full ladder is on Grade). Because capacity follows grade, a low-tier Void piece can out-earn a high-tier Rare one.

Socketed itemWhat it doesTaking it out again
GemAdds a flat amount of the piece’s own stat. Applies the moment it is placed.Comes back to your bag intact whenever the piece leaves its slot, or when you swap another gem in.
RuneAdds a percentage of the piece’s stat (“increased”). Applies the moment it is placed.Same as a gem: it returns loose to the bag.
Enchant scrollRolls an enchantment onto the piece. The scroll’s tier sets the band the number is drawn from and its grade how high in that band it tends to land; the server makes the draw (a reload cannot re-roll it). The enchantment pool and its bands: Stat Tables.Never. A scroll is bound to the piece it was applied to: it stays on it through unequip, class changes and grade promotions, and the only way to change it is to apply another scroll over it, which destroys the old one.
  • The stat has to match. An Attack gem or rune only goes into an Attack piece; a Defence stone only into Defence gear. On a creature tag the base stat counts: an Attack gem fits a Fang (Creature Attack) tag. The socket picker only offers stones that fit.
  • Level requirement. A gem, rune or scroll of tier T needs level (T−1)×10 (T1 = 1): your account level for character gear, the creature’s level for a tag. The picker greys an over-level stone and refuses it before anything is spent; one already in place above level simply counts for nothing until the level is reached.
  • Leaving a slot. Unequipping a piece, letting Auto-Optimize displace it or changing class all do the same thing: the piece goes to the bag, its gems and runes come back loose beside it, and its scroll stays on it. The bag needs room for the piece and every gem and rune coming back, or nothing moves.
  • Changing class empties all eight character gear slots this way at once (both hands, both accessories, four armour). Your creatures’ tags are not touched. A saved build puts a class’s gear back in one click.
  • Re-equipping a piece that carries a scroll puts the scroll back in its socket.
  • Promoting a worn piece at the upgrade bench keeps every stone in place.
  • Dismantling a piece takes its scroll with it; its gems and runes are not in it any more — they came back to the bag when it was unequipped.

Applying a scroll

A gem or a rune applies the moment you pick it. A scroll waits for the server, because its enchantment is a chance outcome and the server draws it. A scroll is destroyed when replaced and can never be taken out, so the order matters:

  1. The stat is read from the piece you wear. If the piece or its stat cannot be read, the bench says so and the scroll is untouched.
  2. The draw belongs to that scroll. A reload, or picking the same scroll again, can never buy it a second draw.
  3. If the server cannot answer, nothing is spent. The scroll stays in your bag; try again.
  4. Only then is the scroll used up. On an empty socket that happens at once. On an occupied one you are asked to confirm first, because the scroll already in the socket is destroyed — gems and runes return to the bag intact, a replaced scroll does not. Cancelling spends nothing.
  5. The result is saved straight away.
