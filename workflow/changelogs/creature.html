2026-10-22 — The server says when a creature evolves (T19, part 2). D, Tower rulings form card T19 (2026-10-18): "Investigate and fix it this round. We are wiping now." Evolution had no server door: the client checked its own level against Evolve_Level, played the reveal and wrote the new form (name, imgId) into the creature's record, and the save door took it (the _advQ claim only flagged a bad one). Now a creature advancement asks POST /api/evolve first (uid, the form it leaves, the form it reaches); the worker stamps the step the line takes off creatures.csv (Evolves_To, Evolve_Level) and the player object judges the creature off its stored record: the current form must be the step's departure (or the ledger must already hold the earlier leg of an S1→S2→S3 chain) and its level — the best of the record's XP, the species map and the items ledger's XP — must reach the bar. The step is written to adv_ledger (src door: the server's record of the creature's stage and the level it fired at, which is what MOVEVER's moveVerAt derives each move's version from) and the items row takes the new form's name. The reveal plays only on the door's answer; a refusal names its cause (evolve_short: the server's level and the bar; evolve_unknown / evolve_form: the stored save is behind this device, so it saves first and asks again). The save door then judges every held creature whose form moved against the stored save: no ledger row for the step is flagged evolve_claim_bad (system class, the auto pile); after the wipe (epoch 2) the form is also put back to the stored one and the correction rides the answer (evo.back) — the save lands, the claim does not (XP409's rule). The class advance has no creature row and does not pass the door. Probe tools/probe_t19b.py on a local worker, 4/13 → 13/13.

2026-10-22 — The step after a cry waits for the cry to end (T15). D, Tower rulings form card T15 (2026-10-18): "Keep 1.2 s. Should not overlap but be sequential." The 1.2 s ceiling stays and no cry is trimmed further. What overlapped: the battle intro's first beat came at a fixed 850 ms and the advancement reveal's new form at a fixed 800 ms after the cry, while a stage-3 cry runs 0.93 s at the median and up to 1.2 s, so most stage-3 cries played past the step and over it. Now the game reads each cry's own length when it plays it and the step that follows waits for the cry to end: the intro's first beat and the reveal's new form come at the cry's end when it is longer than their fixed time, never past the 1.2 s ceiling, and at the fixed time when it is shorter or when no cry plays (audio off, muted, no row). The intro's later beats keep their spacing (they move by the same amount, at most 350 ms); no step comes earlier than before. Probe probe_t15 5/9 → 9/9. Client on DEV.

2026-09-28 — Every creature has its own cry (#993). D: a floor engage "always plays the same high pitch screech". It was one cue, battle.start (floor_arrive.ogg, 96% of its energy above 4 kHz), fired for every fight. The cries follow the old generations' scheme: each LINE has one base cry chosen from its design (a boar grunts, a toad croaks, a serpent hisses, a golem clangs, a ghost moans - 22 kinds, from the Description words and the types; tools/gen_cries.py writes the table), its secondary type colours it (Undead breath, Elemental crackle, Eldritch ring, Horror roughness...), and each STAGE plays it lower (about 3.5 semitones a stage), longer, with more partials, more roughness and a sub-octave - stage 3 is the menacing one. No screech by construction: nothing is synthesized above 3.6 kHz and a 3.8 kHz low-pass closes every cry (worst case 1.8% of energy above 4 kHz, median centroid 463 Hz); each is normalised to the -18 LUFS ceiling and plays at the evolve cue's gain. The game plays the engaged creature's cry on a floor engage (battle.start only as the fallback), the advanced form's cry at the advancement reveal, and a CRY button on the creature sheet. The CRY line above plays all three stages of this line. Client on DEV; this page carries the same synth file.
