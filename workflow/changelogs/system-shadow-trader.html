Lead paragraph

A tab of the Premium Shop, open all week (#770, D 2026-09-07 — LIVE on release since 2026-09-08 (0.71).; weekend-only until then). Every week the trader takes every currency except Wraithgold and hands back one currency — a different one each week — at a rate that is computed from how long each currency takes to earn, never authored by hand. Raise or lower any of them and the rates follow on the next request — a balance change can never leave the trader out of step.
One rate per account: its own best floorT5 (D, 2026-10-18, Tower rulings form: “Rebalance the trader, rebalance the income, but no cap on trader”; Bug Run 0.89-4 Q14). The Worker reads the account’s progress ratchet (its best floor, the number only credited battles can raise; GET /api/_internal/ratchet on the player object) and computes the rates at that floor — time parity plus the 25% cut at the floor the account can actually farm. A boss floor prices at the wild floor below it. No ratchet, no quote (503), never a fallback to 119. There is no weekly cap (D). Superseded rule — D, 2026-08-25: “The shadow trader cannot have a dynamic per account rate. It needs to be the same for everyone, and based off endgame playtime.” Every rate is computed at one reference floor, TRADER_REFERENCE_FLOOR (the endgame), never at the account's own floor. The two candidate floors are tabled below — 99 and 119, the wild floors; 100 and 120 are boss floors with no wild encounters, and the model prices a boss floor at the wild floor below it, so a table headed “floor 100” was floor 99's numbers (D caught the label 2026-08-25). D picked 119 (“119 as a base for the shadow trader”, 2026-08-25): TRADER_REFERENCE_FLOOR = 119.
The cutTRADER_PREMIUM = 0.25: you pay time-parity × 1.25, i.e. you get back 80% of what the same time would have earned you directly. One constant, in src/trader.js; D's to tune.
Whole numbers, no remainderA trade is quoted as pay P of A, receive G of B with both whole. P is exactly what G costs (rounded up to the unit); a request too small to buy one unit is refused, never partially taken.
Server-authoritativeThe client sends lines [{from, amount}] and an idem key, nothing else. The Worker prices every line off the tables; the player's object replays on the key, enforces a 30-second per-account cooldown, validates every line against the stored wallet (a short line refuses with what you hold and what it needs), then writes the exchange into the stored save itself (version bumped) and answers the new balances and version — the client only adopts. The client also saves before asking, so income still only in memory is never overwritten by the adopted wallet. The door sits in the wallet rate class on top of the cooldown.

The model — what one battle yields at floor F

Battles per hour cancel out of every ratio, so “acquisition time” reduces to expected units per battle. One battle at floor F, as the worker runs it:

  • Three kills, each of a rarity drawn from encounter_weights.csv for F.
  • Ichor per kill = round(2 × (1 + min(100, F)/100) × mult[rarity]) — the battle credit's own formula since #772 (2026-09-10), mult from tables.json food_rarity_mult: C 1 / UC 1.1 / R 1.2 / SR 1.35 / SSR 1.5.
  • Three lanes per kill — gear, enhancement, egg — each landing with Chance_<rarity> from drop_chances_by_floor.csv.
  • A gear drop is uniform over the items.csv bases of the floor's tier among Weapon / Armour / Accessory / Tag, graded by the floor's drop_grade_weights band, and dismantles per dismantle.csv (Gear rows; a Tag also returns Essence).
  • An enhancement drop is an even Gem / Rune / Scroll split, graded one band behind (drop_rates.csv Enhancement_Grade_Rule), and dismantles to Dust / Essence / Ink.
  • An egg drop is graded by the band clamped at the wild-egg cap and dismantles to breeding.csv Egg_Dismantle_Ichor.
  • Not modelled, on purpose: bosses, first-win bonuses, keys, shop drops — they are not the steady state a rate should describe.

Then, for the week's target B and any other currency A: rate(A→B) = perBattle(A) ÷ perBattle(B) × (1 + premium), in units of A per one B.

The reference floor — 99 and 119, tabled for D (the current codex, #772 Ichor curve)

Per-battle yields at each candidate floor, the time parity of each currency in Ichor (its weight: how many Ichor the same play time earns instead of one unit of it), and the full parity matrix. The trader's cut (×1.25) goes on top of any matrix cell. D picked 119; the constant is 119. Numbers are acquisitionPerBattle in src/trader.js run on the current codex. The 2026-08-25 simulation (tools/sim_trader.mjs, 300,000 battles per floor, within 1% of the model) predates #772 and still carries the old Ichor ladder; its Ichor column is not re-run.

A note the model surfaced (not changed): drop_rates.csv says each lane lands “exactly 10% per battle (3 kills)”, but the authored per-kill chances, mix-weighted on the encounter weights, come to ~6.7% per kill — about 19% per battle per lane, roughly twice the stated figure.
