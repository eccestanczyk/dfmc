2026-08-02 (b) — BALANCE SHIPPED (D order, silent update). Six numbers, moved together. (1) RiskPremium is GONE — CHAR_RISK 0.15 → 0.00. Character Attack is now (ΣItemStats + 0.25×Core) × ClassRating + 188, symmetric with Defence. D ruling: the classes already carry their identity in ClassRating, and paying it twice off the Defence grade produced a 5.8× spread in gear-to-Attack conversion. (2) CHAR_HP_SQUISH is GONE — 0.12 → 0.00. The same inversion in the HP formula had a best-in-slot Warrior holding LESS HP than a Mage (3,470 vs 4,609); the HP order is now the right way up. (3) Creature power moved off level and onto gear — coreOf k 1.00 → 0.60, and the tag implicits doubled to compensate: Fang and Carapace 27 → 54, Heart 55 → 110 (all 30 tag rows). Why: a creature’s power was level-borne and therefore fully available from turn one, while the avatar’s is gear-borne and starts at nothing — so the two could only ever cross at one point. Making the creature gear-dependent too is what produces parity across the early and mid game rather than at a single tier. Measured, avatar/creature POWER by tier (average of all six classes against the creature a player actually holds at that stage): before 0.72 / 0.74 / 0.70 / 0.85 / 0.96 / 1.06 → after 1.01 / 0.95 / 0.82 / 0.94 / 1.02 / 1.04 for T1–T6. The T3 dip is the stage gates, not the coefficients: creature lines advance at level 24 and classes at 33, so at level 30 the creature is a stage ahead. Gates stay at 33/66 by D’s ruling. Late game is NOT addressed and is deliberately left for review — the avatar still overshoots at the top, and the cause is socket surface: 8 socketable pieces against a creature’s 2 tags, a flat 4× at every grade, taking the avatar from 0% socket-borne power at Worn to 45% at Void. Every number here comes from tools/statmodel.py, validated exact against a live client on Worn T1, Rare T4 and Void T10 (tools/probe_statmodel.py).

2026-08-02 — Balance review (D). Findings recorded; no numbers changed yet. (1) Both defence-inverting terms rejected by D. RiskPremium = 1 + 0.15×(6−DefGrade) on Attack and CHAR_HP_SQUISH in character HP both pay low-Defence classes and tax high-Defence ones, on top of ClassRating, which already separates them. Together: a 7.1× spread in gear-to-Attack conversion (Warrior 1.15, Mage 7.00) and an INVERTED HP order — a best-in-slot Warrior held 3,470 HP against a Mage’s 4,609. (2) Parity is gear completeness, not a constant. The avatar is ~100% gear-derived, a creature carries an innate level curve plus tags, so measured avatar/creature Attack runs ~0.25× mid-game to 1.00× at full best-in-slot. Creature gear share measured 33% (T3) to 55% (T10). (3) The ultimates were never the fault. At parity the shipped ultimate powers already deliver 1.7–1.8× a creature’s ordinary move; solving for that budget returns 170/235/290 against a shipped 180/240/300. The weak-ultimate report was the avatar’s Attack. Mage AoE is the exception, ~1.3× hot. (4) POWER does not measure fighting strength. atk + def + hp/5 is additive; a fight multiplies survivability by damage. Six classes solved to identical POWER give a 2.2× spread in duel index and a strict 5-0…0-5 ladder by tankiness, unchanged at T1, T4 and T10. (5) Stage gates disagree. Classes advance at level 33/66, creature lines at 24/56, so for nine levels at each gate the creature is a stage ahead in move power and parity dips to 0.77× / 0.83×. Model and duel harness: tools/balance_*.py in dfmc-client, validated against the engine to the digit on every measured point.

From the public page: stat aggregation note (original)

Stat aggregation (multiplier model). ItemStat = (implicit + Σ gems + Σ Flat enchants) × (1 + Σ rune % + Σ Increased% enchants) × (1 + (LOCAL soul − 1) + Σ More% enchants). Scrolls roll ONE enchantment on apply. The CLASS comes from codex/enchants.csv (weights Flat 45 / Increased 40 / More 15), and the roll can only take the stat the host item itself carries — a Defense item never rolls Attack. The VALUE comes from codex/scroll_bands.csv (3.5 scroll rework, 2026-09-05): the SCROLL's tier picks the class band (T1 Flat 4–7, Increased 10.0–20.0%, More 12.0–22.0% … T10 Flat 40–76, Increased 13.0–27.0%, More 16.0–29.0%) and the scroll's grade bends the draw toward the top, position = u^(1/K) with K 1.0 (Worn) … 5.0 (Void). The host item's tier and grade do not enter. More is additive with the item's LOCAL soul multiplier rather than a separate factor. Duplicates are allowed and stack inside their bucket. There is no separate reroll action: applying another scroll over a socketed one rerolls it, drawing a fresh class AND value, and the enchantment it replaces is destroyed. Scrolls can never be unsocketed; gems and runes always return to the bag intact. Three maximum More rolls from T10 scrolls reach +87%, flat enchants add into the base band. Character and creature stats and damage: see the formulas block above (Core 0.6 × 7.5 × (level + 25), rating (3g + 5) / 8, divisor 130 / 7.5, flat term 15 + Attack/50, innate floor +188, random band 0.9–1.1). The figures that follow are the July 2026 history of how those numbers were reached (Core k later went 1.0 → 0.60 and RiskPremium and the HP squish were removed, 2026-08-02(b)). Validated fully-maxed: striker vs char wall 15. MAGNITUDE REWORK + BALANCE PACKAGE (July 2026): every item implicit in items.csv was multiplied by 3 and the class multiplier by 2.5 (denominator 20 -> 8), a combined stat scale of 7.5; Core, the innate floor, HP and the damage constants (divisor 130 -> 17.33, flat 2 -> 15) all scale with it, so the rework alone leaves combat identical and only makes the numbers legible. On top of that: Core k 1.6 -> 1.0, tag implicits x3 again (3.0 -> 27.0 total), the innate floor 10 -> 25 pre-scale, the character level term and risk premium above, character HP x1.5 with the squishiness term, and a wild Attack tag budget from floor 11.1, creature mirror 16.0, HP-stacked creature wall 19.3.

From the public page: section 2 Heart paragraph (original)

110 × tier × GF, about 2× the Fang / Carapace grid — multiply the table above by 2.04. A point of Creature Defense is simply worth more than a point of Creature HP: the Defense tag is added to Core before the axis multiplier (1.0–4.375) and quality, while the HP tag is flat. At the old equal coefficient, measured in effective HP against the real damage roll, Carapace beat Heart by 1.46× to 2.66× at every level and every Defense grade tested, so Heart was never a pick. The doubled coefficient buys parity in EFFECTIVE durability, not double the wall: at Defense grade 9 the two are within a point of each other at Lv 40. They now trade places on two axes — low-Defense creatures and early floors favour Heart (grade 2: +40% vs +29%; Lv 10: +17% vs +8%), high-Defense creatures and deep floors favour Carapace (Lv 80: +26% vs +21%). Revised 2026-07-27; the previous rule was that HP is no cheaper per point than any other stat, which is what made Heart a dead item.

From the public page: section 5 intro (original)

One enchantment rolls when a scroll is applied (server /api/roll, kind enchant). No Ink reroll and no reroll action: applying another scroll over a socketed one is the reroll, and the old enchantment is destroyed. The class is drawn from codex/enchants.csv (Flat 45 / Increased 40 / More 15) on the stat the host item carries (Attack, Defense or HP; Creature Attack / Defense / HP on Tags). The value comes from codex/scroll_bands.csv: the scroll's tier picks the band, the scroll's grade bends the draw, position = u^(1/K), K from 1.0 (Worn) to 5.0 (Void). The host item's tier and grade do not enter, and there is no minimum grade. Example columns show the median roll.

Creature axis grades do not rise with stage
parked 2026-08-02, D: "put this in the balancing backlog, not doing it right now"		 Not a missing implementation — an authored asymmetry. Reported by a player as "stats stay the same between stages". Verified three ways. DATA: creatures.csv carries one grade set per LINE, not per stage — Stormfather is 10/4/3/9 at stages 1, 2 and 3, and 99 of 100 lines are identical at every stage (mean Grade_Attack 5.66 / 5.69 / 5.69). CODE: monFromSlot() captures stage and never uses it for a stat; the [0, 0.6, 0.8, 1] stage ladder exists only for souls. CODEX: creature Atk/Def is (Core(level) + ΣTagStats) × AxisMult(grade) × QM with no stage term, while CLASSES get a per-stage row (Prophet 2/5/5 → 2/7/7 → 3/8/9), and the balance notes state that a creature being a stage ahead shows up in move power. Code and codex agree.

If it were taken: giving creatures class parity (+3 attack grades S1→S3) runs through AxisMult = (3g + 5) / 20 as a same-level buff of +64% (grade 3→6), +45% (5→8), +35% (7→10) and +0% for anything already at 10 — so it also compresses the top of the ladder. That is a 300-row creatures.csv change with a DATA_PIN advance in the same commit, and large enough to want tools/balance_*.py run before and after.

Proposed stage spread
codex/proposed/creature_stage_grades.csv		 The rule, calibrated from CORALHYDRA rather than invented. Its authored S1 sits at axis(6)/axis(9) = 0.719 of S3 on Attack and axis(5)/axis(8) = 0.690 on Defense — so S1 is 0.70 × the S3 multiplier, and S2 is the midpoint, 0.85 ×, each snapped to the nearest grade on the same AxisMult = (3g + 5) / 20 curve. Fed back through the rule, CORALHYDRA's S1 comes out 6 / 5 exactly, which is what makes this a derivation and not a guess.

Current grades are kept as S3 for every line, per D. Attack and Defense are the only axes that move: Utility is flat in CORALHYDRA, and Speed must stay flat because the GDD defines it as ungeared, unlevelled and unaffected by quality — a per-stage Speed would silently reorder turns on advancement.

Why not a flat grade delta. CORALHYDRA's S1 is literally S3−3, but the grade distribution is bottom-heavy (23 lines at Attack 3, 27 at Defense 3), so a flat −3 would collapse 63 of 100 lines to the grade 1 floor and erase the role signal that a low axis grade carries. Scaling the multiplier instead clamps only 2 of 100.

Result: mean S1→S3 Attack gain ×1.42 (min ×1.00 on the two floored lines, max ×1.55), landing beside the +45% class ladder rather than on top of it. 199 of 300 rows change; all 100 S3 rows are untouched by construction.

One thing to rule on: CORALHYDRA's authored S2 is flat with S3 (9/8), so its second advancement currently pays nothing. The rule gives it 7/7, making the ladder monotonic and every advancement felt. Taking the rule changes CORALHYDRA at S2 only; keeping the authored value means the last advancement is a move-power upgrade with no stat gain.
