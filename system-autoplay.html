AUTOPLAY & FLOOR TRANSITS

Other Systems › Autoplay & Floor Transits

Autoplay reduces grind: it picks a live creature on the current floor and engages it, over and over, and advances the floor when the portal is open. It is a convenience over the ordinary floor loop — it takes no action a player could not take by hand, and it never enters a boss den.

The loop

  • Runs inside tickFloor(), which only executes while phase === 'floor'. Every other phase (battle, boss den, victory, defeat) stops it dead.
  • After returning to the floor it waits 2600ms, then acts once and re-arms.
  • Advance: if the auto-advance setting is on and the portal is open, it climbs. On a pre-boss floor (floor % 10 === 9) it climbs only if that zone's boss is already down — otherwise it keeps farming. Autoplay never takes a boss; the player does.
  • Engage: otherwise it picks a uniformly random living spawn, sets the pan-to cue, and schedules the engage 650ms later.
  • Overlays (inventory, breeding, equipment) freeze the roam physics for performance but do not stop the engage step — play continues under an open window by design.

Descending on death

Status: on DEV (0.33), not yet on release. The On Death battle option (Stay / Go Down, default Go Down) is read in exactly one place — the autoplay defeat handler. A defeat dismissed by hand never descends, whatever the setting says.

With autoplay on, 2200ms after the defeat scene appears:

  • Lost to a boss: no descent. floorNum already reads the pre-boss floor for the whole boss fight (travelToBossRoom() parks it at bf-1), so the floor below the boss is the one already loaded — the run returns to it. Descending here would have skipped a floor, which it did until 2026-07-31.
  • Floor > 1: descends one floor.
  • Floor 1: stays. There is nothing below it.
  • Any death descent switches Auto-Advance Floors off, persisted, so the climb is re-armed deliberately rather than immediately undoing the retreat.

The den is skipped, autoplay only. A manual descent onto a boss floor always routes into the den (travel() hands off to travelToBossRoom()), mirroring the climb — a downed boss does not skip it. An autoplay descent passes opts.skipBoss, which bypasses that branch; the same clamp that keeps an upward target off a boss floor then carries the target one further down. So 11 → 9, 21 → 19, and an ordinary descent (8 → 7) is untouched.

Why it must be skipped: autoplay never fights bosses, and the loop only runs while phase === 'floor'. A death descent that landed in the den therefore parked the run in a room the loop cannot act in, with no timer and no path back — it stood still until the player noticed and walked out by hand. Reported live 2026-08-05 (feedback #28) while descending repeatedly after losses. Verified by tools/probe_r28.py (8/8; the unpatched build fails three of the eight, including “autoplay resumes on the landed floor”).

Transits, and why the engage is guarded

Changing floor or entering a boss den is a transit: floorTransit is set, an input-blocking veil covers the stage, and every image for the destination is decoded before the new phase is applied. The veil holds for a minimum of 1400ms, with a 30s anti-softlock failsafe.

The phase does not change until the transit lands. Through the whole veil the game still reads phase === 'floor'. Three rules follow, and all three are load-bearing:

  • The autoplay loop does not arm a new engage while floorTransit is set.
  • The queued 650ms engage re-checks !floorTransit when it fires, not only when it is scheduled. A transit that begins inside that 650ms window is the whole bug class.
  • enterBattle() refuses outright while floorTransit is set. This is the central guard: it covers autoplay, a manual click on a creature under the veil, and any future caller.

Autochallenge Boss — a switch on a room, not on the loop

The floor loop never auto-enters a boss den; that is a fixed rule and not a setting, and it is unchanged. Autochallenge Boss is a separate switch that lives inside the den, under the challenge button. Armed, it opens the boss fight as soon as the gravestone clears, fights it, and does so again and again until the boss keys run out. D, 2026-08-07: “It should keep fighting only the boss until you are out of keys.”

  • It fights the boss, and only the boss. Armed, it opens the fight and plays it: moves resolve unattended, the manual menu does not draw, and the victory, power-up and advancement screens advance themselves. It is not floor autoplay — the wild engage in tickFloor() still reads s.autoplay alone, so walking out of the den with the switch on engages nothing.
  • The boss context is the whole gate. autoFighting() is s.autoplay or s.bossAuto while inBossFight (the fight) or vicWasBoss (the victory, power-up and advancement scenes that follow it, set by the boss-win commit and cleared by toFloor()). Every manual-play affordance — move menu, target picker, confirm, ultimate, actor ring — hides on the same test, or the fight would resolve itself underneath a live menu.
  • Leaving the den disarms it. Derived, not a disarm call per door: there are five ways out of a den — bossExit(), travel() up, travel() down, atlas travelTo() and the key's own boss button — and a list of five is wrong the first time a sixth is added. bossAutoLeaveGuard() asks the only question that matters instead: are we still somewhere the switch means anything? The den, the boss fight (inBossFight, covering bintro through resolving) and the scenes after a boss win (vicWasBoss) are the whole of that; anything else and it turns itself off. A transit is not a departure — travel() holds the phase at 'bossroom' while the next floor's art decodes, so the guard fires on arrival, and the loop already refuses while floorTransit is set.
  • The gravestone is still the pacing. Autochallenge waits out BOSS_RESPAWN (10s) exactly as a player would; it does not shorten, reset or bypass it. Keys remain the real rate limit.
  • It refuses to arm with no key for that den (NO BOSS KEYS FOR THIS DEN), and it disarms itself when the last key is spent.
  • An open panel suspends it. Reading the codex or the bag while standing in a den must not end with the player thrown into a boss fight. The test is the derived isPanelOpen() written for the incense pill, so a panel added later is covered without touching this code. A panel is what is on screen (2026-08-26): a sub-window of the bag that only renders while the bag is open — the soul bag's FILTERS window — does not count on its own; the bag's own flag already does. Its flag outlives the bag (nothing clears it on the bag's close), and before this it silently held the switch off for the rest of the session (#580).
  • Out of keys — Stay / Go Up / Go Down. A new Battle & Autoplay setting. The switch is turned off before the trip, so a refused or veiled travel cannot leave the loop armed and re-fire on arrival.

The default splits by realm: Go Down on Eternal, Stay on removed-mode. A removed-mode death is permanent, so a run there never walks itself anywhere on its own authority. This is the first setting whose default depends on the realm: settings were already stored per realm (one save slot each) — only the fallback did not know which realm it was in. optDef() reads defHc for removed-mode and ignores it everywhere else, so an option without one behaves exactly as before, and restoreSection() inherits the split for free because it deletes keys rather than writing defaults.

Why the loop rides the den's repaint instead of its own timer. challengeBoss() calls clearTimers(). A loop built on setTimeout would therefore destroy itself on its first challenge and never fire again — the same shape as the gravestone freeze in report #18, where the room sat frozen because the fight had cleared the timer that repainted it. Autochallenge instead hangs off the 400 ms tick the den already runs, which nothing in the battle path can cancel.

Client: bossAutoTick(), bossAutoOut(), toggleBossAuto(), optDef() in play/app.js. Probe: tools/probe_bossauto.py (18/18).

Failure mode this prevents

Reported live 2026-08-01 (feedback #12) and reproduced against the reporter's own save. With autoplay on, an engage armed 650ms before the player entered the boss den. The den transit began; the phase was still 'floor'; the engage fired and started an ordinary floor battle. enterBattle() calls clearTimers(), which destroyed the den transit's own pending apply — including its 30s failsafe — so floorTransit stayed set permanently. The veil never lifted, the den was never reached, and the boss challenge button underneath stayed live: the player spent a Key from that wedged state and got the standard fight. Keys are the rarest lane in a zone, so the loss stranded him on the floor.

Client: tickFloor() autoplay block, enterBattle(), enterBossRoom(), travel() in play/app.js

The Ultimate setting — Turn 1 / Turn 2 / Turn 3 / Never Use

One segmented setting decides when autoplay fires the class ultimate. Turn-N semantics: the ult fires on the first legal round at or after N — if it is denied there (echo with nothing slotted, unit dead), autoplay takes a normal move and retries every round after. Never Use never queues it. Default is Turn 1, the old behavior.

  • The retired value migrates without a write. Accounts that stored the pre-3.8 First Turn read back — and behave as — Turn 1; the engine treats any unknown value as 1 for the same reason: a stored settings snapshot must never change behavior on a string.
  • This is the Auto PVP defense's brain too. The same picker (module autoQueue) pilots the account's defense with the owner's settings, so the timing chosen here is the timing attackers meet.

The move picker — what autoplay chooses each round

The same picker (module autoQueue) drives PvE autoplay and every account’s Auto PVP defense. Per unit, per round:

  1. The ultimate, on the round the Ultimate setting names, retrying every round after.
  2. Prioritize Debuffs (setting, off by default): the first ready debuff whose status is missing on some living enemy.
  3. Prioritize Buffs (setting, off by default): the first ready buff whose status is missing on some ally it would reach.
  4. The base pick, among the ready damaging moves. Brain 2 (ON DEV since 2026-09-02, LIVE on release since 2026-09-02 (0.66).): a move whose effect is already fully applied — a buff on every unit it would buff, or a debuff on every living enemy — sorts last; then longer cooldown first, whatever it hits for; then average damage; then slot order. Nothing is ever skipped: there is no pass, so if the only ready move is a fully-applied debuff, that is the move. Brain 1 (release): the highest average damage, every round.

Why brain 2 exists: a no-cooldown move that out-hit the slot beside it won the base pick every round, so the cooldown move never fired (“my 4th slot does nothing on auto”). D’s ruling: cooldown beats higher damage. The brain is a field on the pinned defense settings, brain; see Auto PVP for why it rides the snapshot.
