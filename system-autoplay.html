AUTOPLAY & FLOOR TRANSITS

Other Systems › Autoplay & Floor Transits

Autoplay fights for you: it picks a live creature on the current floor and engages it, over and over, and climbs when the portal is open. It takes no action you could not take by hand, and it never enters a boss den.

When you get it

Autoplay opens when you win your first battle (a lost or abandoned fight does not count; floor 1). Until then the AUTOPLAY button on the floor shows a padlock and reads OPENS AFTER YOUR FIRST BATTLE; an arrow points at it once it opens. Press the button (or the A key) to switch it on or off.

Its settings live under Settings > Battle & Autoplay; the cogwheel beside the AUTOPLAY button opens that section. They are part of your save and follow you between devices.

The loop

  • It runs only while you are on the floor. A battle, a boss den, or a victory or defeat screen pauses it.
  • Back on the floor, it waits 2.6 seconds, acts once, then waits again.
  • Climb: if Auto-Advance Floors is on and the portal is open, it climbs. On the floor just below a boss it climbs only if that boss is already beaten; otherwise it keeps farming. Autoplay never takes a boss; you do.
  • Engage: otherwise it picks a random living creature, pans to it, and engages it a moment later.
  • Opening a window (bag, breeding, equipment) does not stop it: play carries on underneath.
  • It never engages while the floor is changing, so a trip to a new floor or into a boss den is never interrupted by a fight.

Descending on death

The On Death setting (Stay / Go Down, default Go Down) only acts when autoplay is on. A defeat you dismiss yourself never moves you.

With autoplay on and On Death set to Go Down, 2.2 seconds after the defeat screen appears:

  • Lost to a boss: you return to the floor below the boss, the floor you came from. No further descent.
  • Floor 2 or above: you go down one floor.
  • Floor 1: you stay. There is nothing below it.
  • Every such death on floor 2 or above, boss losses included, switches Auto-Advance Floors off, so you choose when to climb again instead of walking straight back into the loss.
  • The den is skipped. An autoplay descent onto a boss floor passes its den and lands one floor further down: 11 → 9, 21 → 19. An ordinary descent (8 → 7) is unchanged. Descending by hand always takes you through the den.

With On Death set to Stay, autoplay takes you back to the floor you fought on (after a boss loss, the floor below the boss) and carries on.

Autochallenge Boss

The floor loop never enters a boss den; that is a fixed rule, not a setting. Autochallenge Boss is a separate switch inside the den, under the challenge button. When it is on, it challenges the boss as soon as the gravestone clears, fights the battle for you, and repeats until your boss keys for that den run out.

  • It fights the boss, and only the boss. Moves resolve on their own and the victory, power-up and advancement screens move on by themselves. Walk out of the den with it on and nothing on the floor is engaged; that is floor autoplay’s job.
  • Leaving the den turns it off, whichever way you leave. Losing to the boss turns it off too.
  • The gravestone still sets the pace. It waits out the boss’s 10-second return exactly as you would. Keys remain the real limit.
  • It will not switch on without a key for that den (NO BOSS KEYS FOR THIS DEN), and it switches itself off when the last key is spent.
  • An open window pauses it. Reading the codex or the bag in a den never ends with you thrown into a boss fight.
  • Out of keys: the Autochallenge Boss — Out of Keys setting decides what happens next: Stay, Go Up or Go Down (default Go Down). The switch turns off before the trip.

The Ultimate setting

One setting decides when autoplay fires your class ultimate: Turn 1, Turn 2, Turn 3 or Never Use (default Turn 1).

  • Turn N fires the ultimate on the first round at or after N where it can be used. If it cannot fire that round, autoplay takes a normal move and tries again every round after.
  • Never Use never fires it.
  • An attacking ultimate is aimed by your Targeting setting.
  • Your Async PvP defense is piloted by the same picker with your own settings, so the timing you choose here is the timing attackers meet.

The move picker — what autoplay chooses each round

The same picker drives your autoplay and your Async PvP defense. Per creature, per round, the first rule that applies wins:

  1. The ultimate, on the round the Ultimate setting names, retrying every round after.
  2. Prioritize Debuffs (off by default): the first ready debuff that some living enemy does not have yet, aimed at one of the enemies still missing it. Lowest / Highest Health picks among them by current HP; Random rolls a fresh one each time.
  3. Prioritize Buffs (off by default): the first ready buff that some ally it would reach does not have yet.
  4. The base pick, among the ready damaging moves: a move whose effect is already fully applied (a buff on every unit it would buff, or a debuff on every living enemy) goes last; then the longer cooldown first, whatever it hits for; then higher average damage; then slot order. Nothing is ever skipped: if the only ready move is a fully applied debuff, that is the move. The enemy is chosen by Targeting: Lowest / Highest Health by current HP, Random a fresh roll per creature per round.
  • Cooldown beats damage so that a strong move with no cooldown cannot hog every round while your cooldown move never fires.
  • A move on cooldown is never picked. A creature whose every move is on cooldown skips the round.
  • If the target dies earlier in the round, the attack re-aims by the same Targeting rule among the enemies still standing. In Async PvP both sides do this. In manual play, and for wild enemies, the attack falls to the first enemy in line instead.
